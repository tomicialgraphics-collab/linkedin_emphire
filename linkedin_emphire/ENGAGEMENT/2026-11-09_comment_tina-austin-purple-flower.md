# Comment on Tina Austin's purple flower post, 9 November 2026

## Why this angle

She has already made the case that access is not the whole story and that judgment is, so agreeing with her would be noise on a post that has plenty of it.

The opening is that her relevance result and her purple flower are the same failure seen from two directions. A passing mention promoted to a substantial discussion is a sentence ranked too high. A limitation dropped is a sentence ranked too low. Both come from ranking by emphasis inside the paper rather than by what the sentence does to the conclusion. Naming that symmetry turns two observations into one mechanism, and then into a test she can run in a classroom on Monday.

The field specific answer to her question has to be real rather than decorative. In security work the detail that changes meaning is the negative result, because it is written flatly and carries no claim.

## Comment

The purple flower in security work is the negative result. The paper that reports what did not work, and the line in the discussion that says what the study did not test. Both are low on emphasis and high on information, so the summary keeps the claim and drops the boundary. I have watched a review turn "we found no effect here" into "evidence of no effect", which are opposite statements.

That is the mirror of your relevance finding. A passing mention promoted to a substantial discussion and a stated limitation demoted to nothing are the same error: sentences ranked by how forcefully they are written rather than by what they do to the conclusion.

Which suggests a cheap test. Ask for the limitations before the findings and see whether the summary changes. If it does, the reader was being shown the paper's emphasis, not its argument.

## What not to say

No restatement of her three findings. No compliment in the first line, since a post about judgment attracts plenty of those and they cost her nothing to read and teach her less. No mention of my own work or of evaluation tooling, because her audience is educators and the point has to stand without a product behind it. No answer that is only about my field, since the question is general and the security example is evidence rather than the subject.

## Held for a later turn

If she replies, the useful extension is what a rubric for salience would even look like, because the reason this fails is that nobody has written down which sentences carry weight in a paper. That is closer to her discipline than mine.
