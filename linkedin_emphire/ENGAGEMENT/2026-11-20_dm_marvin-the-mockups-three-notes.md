## Message, paste as is

<!-- gate:body:start -->
Read all nine pages twice. The placeholders are the best thing in it. No invented postcode, no invented transaction id, a QR that says it does not scan, and the issuing office left open. Most design packs are full of numbers that turn out to be fiction, and this one refuses. Listing the five states you haven't drawn is the same instinct, and it's why I can read this in ten minutes instead of an hour.

Three notes, in the order they would cost us.

Screen nine says nothing has been changed since. That's true today and false the first time we seal a second batch, which REQ-030 allows. Make it per batch: three items sealed on this date, nothing sealed at that time has changed since, and a later batch appears as its own line. Then the sentence stays true for the life of the file.

The green panel with a tick reads as approval, and the sentence that stops it being read that way is at the foot in small grey. Put that sentence beside the badge, before the item list. Keep the green for the one narrow thing it earns, which is that this batch is unchanged.

Part three is bigger than it looks. The four results are drawn on a postcode check, which is the register. Our public check page is a file check, reached by QR. Both are right, and they are not the same screen, and only one of them is inside the forty-five. I'll write the requirement that says which, before anyone builds either.

The Yoruba is mine. I'll do the pass this evening, including whether "bá a mu" wants to be "bá ara wọn mu" and the two places where "olùṣàyẹ̀wò ẹ̀rí sí i" reads oddly.

One small thing that isn't small. The header says Ẹ̀rí and the brand field says Ẹrí. That field is what every screen reads, so it's two products depending on which page loads. One string, in the brand file.
