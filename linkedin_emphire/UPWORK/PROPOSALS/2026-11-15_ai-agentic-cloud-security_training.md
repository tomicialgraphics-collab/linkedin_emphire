# AI Agentic Cloud Security, $12 to $25 an hour, Dallas, 7 Connects

**Verdict: send it.** The profile that would have been the wall behind this proposal now reads as the right trade in the right words: software engineer first, AI red teaming in the title, $65 an hour, two jobs completed. The posting asks for an AI agentic security practitioner who can give personal trainings, and that is what the page now says.

## Before sending, two clicks

**Set hours per week.** The field is empty on the live page, and this client filters on availability because the posting caps at under thirty hours a week.

**Send at $45/hr with the hours capped at eight.** Their own sentence invites it: they say they will pay higher for the most experienced person, and their band is the platform default arguing with that sentence. A $65 profile quoting $25 is the incoherent choice. If winning matters more than the rate, the same text at $25 works.

## The proposal, paste this

<!-- gate:body:start -->
You said you would pay more for the most experienced person, so I will be straight about the price and short about the pitch.

I red-team AI agents. Injection that crosses tool boundaries, MCP servers used as a foothold, credentials pulled out through an agent chain, non-human identity. I keep the test set in the open, so what I teach is the thing I run.

How I would work: one paid session against something real of yours, an agent you built or one you want to be able to trust. Then a written plan built from whatever actually broke. Then sessions against that plan until you can run the tests yourself.

Eight hours, capped, so the total stays predictable and you are not buying my learning curve.

One thing shapes the first session. Whether this is for your own capability or for a team you have to bring up, tell me and I will bring the right material.
<!-- gate:body:end -->

**Rate: $45/hr, capped at 8 hours, roughly $360.** Their posting says they will pay higher for the most experienced freelancer, and their own band is a default that contradicts that sentence. Quoting $25 with a $65 profile rate is the incoherent choice, and quoting $45 uses their sentence against their band rather than against you.

**If the win matters more than the rate**, which for a first completed contract it might, send the same text at $25 and keep the cap. The proposal does not change.

## What this job is actually worth

Not the money. Eight hours at $45 is $360, which is 7% of the gap to 25 December and would take a week of evenings to earn.

It is worth **a third completed contract and a review**, since two are already on the record and the third is what moves the account toward the reviewed tier where the higher rates open. It is also worth a curriculum: the material for training an agentic-security practitioner is the same material as the playbook, and written once it is a product that can be sold again without the hours.

## The client, read from the numbers

**In favour.** Fewer than 5 proposals, so the text gets read. Expert level with a phone verified. It is a real subject that matches the work exactly, and "personal trainings" is a request for judgement, which is the thing that is hard to hire.

**Against.** Member since 14 November 2026, an account opened the day before the job was posted, with three jobs on it and a 0% hire rate. Payment method not verified. A brand new account posting three jobs at once is often a person testing the platform, a recruiter gathering CVs, or someone about to discover what senior security time costs. Expect a no, and treat a reply as a bonus.

**The one thing not to do** is start work before the contract exists or accept an off-platform arrangement, which is also a terms violation and would cost the account it is trying to build.
