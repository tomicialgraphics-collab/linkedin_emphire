What was wrong first, and was corrected

My register opened with a comparison that counted a test file's vectors as evidence of coverage. The author
showed it counted a command he had not run, and the sentence was withdrawn rather than softened. A second
claim, that a specific subsystem was closed, lost its heading and became a registered item instead. A third
correction was mine alone: two commits were named without saying which was which.

Three corrections, two of them raised by the author and one found by re-reading my own text. The register
carries a revision history for exactly this reason: a reviewer who reports only the corrections he was forced
into is not keeping a record. Version 7's own header records a separate set, three editorial fixes applied in
that version, the label, a stale count and the explanation of two counts. The two threes are not the same
three, which is exactly the sort of sentence that has to be written down rather than remembered.

What has changed in the project since

The project closed every item on the register in code or in text, which is the rarer half. One exception, W-04:
it is closed in the text, and the author's own status record says he has not re-run my probe against the rule
that now states the mapping. So it is closed in the specification and not yet confirmed against my test, and
that distinction is his, stated in his record before I asked for it. The verifier specification has moved from v0.10 to v0.14 and each revision names the finding it
answers. A correction raised in the last exchange, that one amendment sentence still described the segments
serialiser as unbuilt after it had been built, is closed in both places: Batch Anchoring Amendment v0.26
corrects the sentence, keeps the old wording visible as superseded, and points at the verifier rule; the
specification now points at v0.26 instead of promising a revision that already exists.
