# 1,007 to 5,000 before 25 December

The ask: 5,000 followers by 25 December, from 1,007 on 6 November.

Everything below is built on numbers already in this repo or said by the owner. Where the repo has no
number, it says so, and the plan starts by taking that measurement, because the whole thing turns on
one question the record cannot currently answer.

---

## 1. The arithmetic, with no cushion

| | |
|---|---|
| Followers on 6 November | 1,007 |
| Target | 5,000 |
| Net new needed | 3,993 |
| Days available, 6 Nov to 25 Dec | 49 |
| **Net new per day** | **81.5** |
| Net new per week | 571 |

Eighty two net new followers a day, every day, for seven weeks. Not eighty two on a good day. The
average is the target.

## 2. What the record actually says

- **90-day review, 21 June to 18 September** (`analytics/2026-09-18_90-day_review.md`): 1,644
  impressions in the whole quarter, 31 engagements, engagement rate 1.9 per cent, best single post 683
  impressions. One outlier at 4,443.
- **485 followers on 31 October** (`STRATEGY/2026-10-31_icp-goals_push-plan.md`,
  `PLANS/2026-11-02_goals_month-week-day.md`, both written to that figure).
- **1,007 followers on 6 November.**

So: 522 net new followers in six days, or about 87 a day, after months of roughly four a day.

**The target is already running ahead of the required rate.** On the last six days' pace, 5,000 arrives
with room to spare. On the pace of June to October, the same 49 days produce about 1,200 total and the
target is unreachable.

Everything depends on one thing. Is the last week a new baseline, or a spike that has already
finished?

And here is the problem. Nothing in this repo records which post, comment or thread produced those 522.
There is no impression count for the 4 November or 6 November posts, no record of which comment
travelled, no daily follower line. The single most important growth event of the year happened and left
no trace to copy.

**That is the first job on 6 November, before any tactic.** You cannot repeat what you did not record.

## 3. Three scenarios, named so you can tell which one you are in

| Scenario | What is true | 25 December | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Repeat | the last six days were a real step change and the causes can be repeated weekly | 5,000 to 5,400 | target met |
| Decay | the spike was one thread, it fades over two weeks, then 20 to 30 a day | 2,200 to 2,600 | target missed, account doubled |
| Baseline | it was a one-off and the old rate returns | about 1,200 | target missed badly |

The plan below is designed to move you from the third row to the first. It cannot promise the first
row. Anybody who promises it from 1,644 impressions a quarter is selling something.

## 4. The five levers, in order of followers per hour

Ranked by what this repo already shows, not by what growth advice usually says.

**1. Comments on the right accounts. Highest rate, and it is already proven here.**
The recorded wins in `STRATEGY/2026-10-12_comment-engine_playbook.md`: a single comment at 8,384
impressions, another at 1,264, a post teaser at 594 with 3 profile viewers and 1 follower. A comment on
a large in-lane account reaches the account's audience without needing your own reach. This is the
lever that built the first thousand and it is the one that can be scaled hardest, because it does not
depend on the algorithm liking your post.
Rate to beat: 5 comments a day, every one on an account above 10,000 followers in the eval or security
lane.

**2. One recurring series, so there is a reason to follow rather than a reason to like.**
Single posts get liked. A series gets followed, because the reader wants the next one. You already have
the raw material: one metric or evaluation per week, taken apart, with the definition problem at the
centre. Same structure, numbered, same day of the week. Three posts a week, one of them always the
series.

**3. Profile conversion. Where a spike becomes followers.**
Followers equal reach times profile visits times follow rate. At 1,007 the bottleneck is no longer
reach alone, it is what a visitor sees in four seconds: headline, banner, first line of About, and the
featured item. `LINKEDIN/about_section_2026-10-24_draft.md` and
`LINKEDIN/banner_wording_2026-11-03.md` already exist and need finalising, not rewriting.

**4. Borrowed audiences. Slowest to start, largest per event.**
A podcast, a newsletter, a guest post, a co-authored piece. One appearance in front of 20,000 people
does what a month of posting cannot. Lead time is weeks, which is why the ask goes out now or never.

**5. Premium, now that you are past 1,000.**
Your own rule kept Premium off below 1k. You crossed it. Premium buys the full viewer list, InMail and
connect notes, and the viewer list is the best targeting data on the platform: it tells you who is
already looking, so the next comment goes where someone is already interested.

## 5. The seven weeks

Targets assume the repeat scenario holds and the levers compound. The checkpoints are the honest part:
they say when to stop pretending.

| Week | Dates | Net new | Running total | The one thing that must happen |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 6 to 12 Nov | 350 | 1,357 | find what produced the 522 and do it three more times |
| 2 | 13 to 19 Nov | 450 | 1,807 | the series has two published parts, profile conversion fixed |
| 3 | 20 to 26 Nov | 550 | 2,357 | first borrowed audience booked, not pitched |
| 4 | 27 Nov to 3 Dec | 600 | 2,957 | one comment above 10,000 impressions |
| 5 | 4 to 10 Dec | 650 | 3,607 | second borrowed audience lands |
| 6 | 11 to 17 Dec | 700 | 4,307 | one post above 50,000 impressions |
| 7 | 18 to 25 Dec | 700 | 5,007 | hold the rate through the holiday lull |

**Checkpoints, and what they mean.**

- **15 November, under 1,600:** the 5,000 target is arithmetically gone. Decision that day, not on 20
  December: accept a stretch target of 2,500 and put the saved hours into the Aithos follow-up and the
  client work, or go all in on borrowed audiences.
- **29 November, under 2,600:** the series is not working as a conversion device. Change the series,
  not the effort.
- **13 December, under 3,300:** stop posting more. Take the five best-performing posts and put paid
  behind the two that brought followers, if and only if the owner is willing to spend.

No target moves without the number being written down next to it.

## 6. The daily routine, time boxed

The 15 minute feed triage rule in `PIPELINE.md` still holds. Growth work is a separate block, and it
is 75 minutes, not a day.

| When | What | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| 07:00 | 5 comments on named accounts, drafted the night before | 40 |
| 12:30 | reply to every comment on the previous day's post, statements not questions | 15 |
| 13:30 | publish on post days, first comment at T+20 | 5 |
| 20:00 | the follower log line, and the three sources that brought them | 5 |
| 21:00 | draft the next morning's five comments from the day's feed | 10 |

**The follower log is not optional.** `analytics/follower-log.md` exists and has one row filled. One
line a day, five fields. Without it, week three is guesswork and the checkpoints cannot be read.

## 7. What would get the number and lose the account

- **Bought followers, pods, engagement groups.** Fast, cheap, and visible to exactly the people you
  want to work with. The account reads as inflated and the InMail response rate falls. Not doing it.
- **"Follow me for more" and follower-count posts.** They convert scrollers, not practitioners, and
  the audience you need is 200 named people, not 5,000 strangers.
- **Generic AI news commentary.** It is the one thing every account in this space does, and it brings
  the audience that reads AI news rather than the audience that hires evaluation work.
- **Chasing volume at the cost of the lane.** 5,000 practitioners in evaluation and security is worth
  more than 50,000 general readers, and the plan above is built to add the first without importing the
  second. If the numbers and the lane ever fight, the lane wins. That is the owner's own rule, applied
  to growth.
