## Comment, paste as is

Ask one more question than those three, and it decides whether the rollout survives: when this tool is wrong, how would you find out, and what would you do next? Teams answer that one honestly, and the answers are the first real risk register the project gets.

The three as written find where work is slow. They do not find where work is consequential. A team asked what wastes its time will hand over the repetitive tasks, which is right, and will stay quiet about the decisions it would rather not see automated, which is rational. Nobody volunteers the process that gets blamed on them when a model gets it wrong.

Consent early is worth having, and it is not the same thing as design input. The people in that first conversation are rarely in the room when the failure pattern shows up, and they are the ones who inherit it.

## What not to say

No agreement preamble about how important people are to adoption, which is her point and is correct. No reference to her book, no link to the newsletter, no framework and no four letter acronym. Nothing that reads as a consultancy offering to run the workshop she just described. The addition has to be a question she did not ask, in the same plain register she used.

## Why the slot is worth it

She writes to the people who sign off on these purchases, and the comment gives them a question that costs nothing to ask and produces something they do not currently have before signing. That is a more useful thing to have said under her post than agreement, and it puts the failure question in front of a leadership audience it usually never reaches.
