# Audience leverage: should the big-number accounts be worked

Written 14 November 2026, after a week of measured feed data. What it answers is whether commenting under high-follower accounts is the way to build the audience, and the short answer is no, not as a growth strategy. It is a research tool with a narrow use.

Two facts sit in that table. Reach comes from posts that open on an event, and it does not come from craft alone. Seventy-seven people saw the Playbook launch, the most substantial artifact of the week. Eight thousand saw a reading of a company.

Then the comment data. A reply tonight in a live thread under a first-degree connection's post recorded **15 impressions**. Replies in other people's threads run at the same order of magnitude. A comment is not a broadcast. It is visible to the author, a few of their readers, and almost nobody else.

## So what do the big accounts actually give you

Three narrow things, and none of them is reach in the sense people mean.

**A research surface.** The comments under a large account's post are a census of what their audience does not know. Reading fifty of them tells you which question to answer in your own post on Monday. That is mining, and the ore is the question, not the eyeball.

**A credibility transfer, when the author replies.** Sébastien's recommendation did more for standing than a thousand impressions, because a named person with a corpus attached said something specific and true. That is what converts. It is also not scalable by commenting on strangers, because strangers do not vouch.

**A repost, occasionally.** One repost by an account with standing is worth more than a hundred comments, and it happens when the post is quotable, not when the comment is clever.

## Bottleneck is not traffic

With 1,234 followers, the constraint is not the number of people who see a post. It is what happens to the small number who click through. If a reader lands on the profile and finds a headline, a testimonial and no artifact, the visit is wasted and every impression spent buying it is wasted with it.

Fix the conversion before buying traffic. Featured holds the Playbook and the audit pack, in that order, with one line each saying what the reader gets. That is an evening's work and it multiplies everything else.

## Working rule

Comment where the author is in the ICP or the audience is, and never for reach alone. Concretely: Brad Wolfe's finance readers, Ricky Jones's authority-boundary crowd, Ron Brazil's forensics people, Sébastien's evidence people. Those are small audiences with high density, which is the opposite of a big audience and the right shape.

One comment per post, inside the first half hour if the post matters, and it has to add a method rather than agreement. Everything else is noise that costs twenty minutes a time.

## What the same two hours buys instead

- One event-anchored post with an artifact in the first comment. The 17 November earnings call is already sitting there.
- Three messages to named people with a reason to answer, which is how the two paid leads this week arrived.
- One artifact improved, because the artifacts are what get cited after the impression count is forgotten.

## Where this leaves the week

A week of commenting under other people's posts produced this week's two best conversations, with Ron and with the woman examining the benchmark, and neither came from a big audience. They came from being early, specific and right in a small room. Watching big accounts for what their readers ask pays. Performing for them does not. They are not worth performing for.
