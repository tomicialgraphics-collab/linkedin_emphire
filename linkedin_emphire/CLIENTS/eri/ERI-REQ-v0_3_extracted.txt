Business Context
Ẹ̀ rí (a working name) is a practice build: a small prototype that Olusanya Timothy and Marvin Percival
build together over four weekly cycles, to learn how they work together before the full register. It could
later be shown at a future “Built on Nigeriaʼs Postcodeˮ pitch session. It starts a tamper-evident
evidence file for a building with a NIPOST postcode. The file holds voice testimony, photos and
documents. Each item is attested by a named person other than the speaker, fingerprinted on the
phone, and anchored publicly through FORAY. A one-page certificate with a QR code lets anyone
check the file. The prototype is the first working slice of the Lagos property evidence register.
User Roles
Verifier: signs in with a Stele passkey; creates evidence files, records items, confirms consent,
records attestations, seals files and switches the public names setting.
Speaker: gives voice testimony; has no account.
Attestor: confirms another personʼs item by voice or typed statement; has no account.
Public checker: opens a certificate or public check page without signing in.
Scope
The prototype covers: verifier sign-in; postcode entry and NIPOST Lookup; evidence files linked to one
postcode; voice, photo and document capture; spoken consent; on-device fingerprints; append-only
storage; attestation and counter-attestation; sealing and FORAY anchoring; a printable certificate with
a QR code; a public check page; English and Yoruba interface text; brand configuration; demo seed
and reset.
Exclusions
Any statement that a title is good, that a property is safe to buy, or who owns a property
Land parcels, survey outlines or coordinates as the evidence fileʼs key
Payments, prices, plans or subscriptions
Collecting, storing or sending NIN numbers or email addresses

Glossary
Evidence file: the record for one NIPOST postcode, holding items and attestations.
Item: one piece of evidence: a voice recording, a photo or a document image.
Speaker: the person whose voice is in a recording item.
Attestor: a named person, other than the speaker, who confirms an item.
Attestation: an attestorʼs confirmation of an item, with relationship (family, neighbour,
independent) and kind (presence, knowledge, independent, verifier).
Counter-attestation: an attestation that disputes an item; added as a new event.
Verifier: a signed-in user who runs capture and sealing.
Fingerprint: the SHA256 hash of an itemʼs content.
Seal: the action that sends a batch of fingerprints to FORAY.
Anchor reference: the transaction ID and block time returned for a sealed batch.
Certificate: the one-page summary of a sealed evidence file, with a QR code.
Public check page: the page anyone can open to see a fileʼs sealed items and test a file against
them.
Postcode: NIPOSTʼs 11-character, 5-segment code for a building.
Assumptions
NIPOST grants API access in time. Test keys do not resolve live postcodes; live keys need
company verification KYB.

The FORAY anchoring service accepts batches of fingerprints and returns a transaction ID and

Stele can be mounted into the prototypeʼs server for passkey sign-in.
Two demo phones are available: an Android phone running Chrome and an iPhone running Safari

Four weekly cycles, then a recorded demo and a joint review.

Non-Functional Requirements
Performance
The NIPOST address appears within 3 seconds of submitting a postcode on a 4G connection, in
at least 9 of 10 timed lookups REQ041.
Security
All traffic uses TLS 1.2 or higher REQ042.
The NIPOST API key never leaves the server REQ006.
Verifiers sign in with passkeys; there are no passwords REQ004.
Scalability
The prototype serves one demo verifier and up to 20 public checkers at once.
Availability
The staging URL is online during each Friday review and the final demo.
Backup and Recovery
The demo can be restored to its seeded state with one command REQ040.
Demo data is deleted within 30 days after the session REQ043.

iPhone Safari
