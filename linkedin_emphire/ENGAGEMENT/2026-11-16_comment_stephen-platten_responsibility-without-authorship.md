## Comment, paste as is

Marcus Aurelius is enforceable on a sentence you wrote yourself, and that's the part that changes.

If it is not true, do not say it assumes the writer knew what was true and chose the words. A generated draft breaks that chain: the sentence exists before anyone believes it, and the writer's job moves from authorship to approval. Responsibility stays the same size. What it sits on shrinks to whether it sounded right.

For a tester there's a version of his rule that isn't about style. Take the report you're about to send and find the sentences you could defend one at a time: the number, the run it came from, whether the check could have failed. What's left over is a claim in your name that you didn't make.

That's the test I'd put in front of a room, and it doesn't care whether a person or a model typed it. Fluency was never the risk. The risk is a sentence that reads well and can't be traced to anything.

## Why this and not the writing question

His question, are we losing our voice, is a question about people, and the answers under a post like this will be about voice, tone and personal style. The comment moves it to duty, because that is what the Marcus Aurelius line is actually about and it is the part a tester can act on.

That three-part check, number, run, whether the check could have failed, is the same test in miniature that the whole audit method rests on. It is also the thing his audience can run on the next report they write, which is the reason to offer it rather than a view.

Both are the same instinct applied to a different document, and neither repeats the other, which matters because he will notice if the same line arrives twice.
