## 1. Sam Illingworth, the children's consultation

Post: 14,000 children surveyed by Savanta for the Department for Science, Innovation and Technology,
seven participatory events, and a report that names mandatory labelling of AI generated content as the
single most consistent practical demand. Labelling, watermark and provenance appear nowhere in the
government response or in the 23 speeches of the Lords debate that followed, and the measure the
children did get was a social media ban that most of them did not want.

Draft, paste as a comment.

---

You fixed the corpus before you searched it, which is what makes an absence citable. Documents plus 23 speeches is a search space somebody else can re-run, so the words not appearing reads as a finding rather than a flourish.

The ask underneath it is cheap. Publish the disposition of every recommendation that came out of the Hack events, taken up or not, with the reason beside it. One table. It also stops this being one department's omission and turns it into something checkable across every consultation, instead of being rediscovered by whoever reads the annex next.

What makes the gap stark is which way round it went. Labelling is the demand that would have required the platforms to move, and the ban is the measure that asks nothing of them. A disposition table would have forced a sentence about that.

---

institutions document what they did. The table is a real ask rather than a wish, and it is the part he

## 2. Iyinoluwa Samuel Aboyeji, Nigerian startup filings

Post: an opaque market is no market at all, the SEC should review venture capital legislation and
require transparent annual filings from Nigerian startups immediately, investors are being damaged,
and enforcement in Delaware costs too much.

Draft, paste as a comment.

---

The filing is the easy half and it is still worth having. What makes an annual filing useful is that two of them can be put side by side, and that only works if the definition travels with the number. Revenue, GMV and total payment volume are the same word in most Nigerian decks and three different figures in the filing.

So the ask is a schema before it is a duty. Who signs it, what period it covers, which figure is meant, defined once for everybody. Without that the commission gets a shelf of documents and the market gets the appearance of transparency with none of the comparison.

Your second point is the one I would push hardest. Delaware is expensive because remedy is expensive, and a filing duty does not make remedy cheaper. It makes noticing cheaper. That is still worth buying, and it is worth saying out loud which of the two is on offer.

---

158 words. The revenue, GMV, TPV distinction is the live one in Lagos fintech and it is the reason a
filing duty fails quietly. The second half takes his strongest point and refines it rather than
agreeing with it, which is what keeps a reply from a stranger worth reading.

## 3. Allie K. Miller, the agent that emailed four friends

Post: OpenAI's dot given a 500 dollar budget and almost no rules for a Saturday. It booked a stamp
workshop, a gallery and dinner at a place neither of them had heard of. It emailed four friends
instead of texting them, and it kept asking the same restaurant for a table until she apologised. Her
note before round two: ask a place twice then move on, text friends and email businesses, and check
before anything that costs money or speaks for her.

Draft, paste as a comment.

---

The budget worked and the two things that broke could not be counted by it, which is the useful part.

Emailing four friends is a missing map rather than a missing rule. A cap is measurable, a channel is not, so the instruction has to sit on the contact: text this one, email that one. Same shape for the restaurant. Ask twice then move on is a timeout, and a timeout is a budget on attempts instead of money.

Written in a note, both depend on you remembering to write them for the next place. Tied to the thing itself, three tries and stop on the restaurant, the agent cannot spend the evening on a table, and the next friend list arrives with the channels already on it.

---

130 words. She documents her own failures, so the comment does not need to point them out. It names the
category error instead, that a budget bounds spend and says nothing about medium or repetition, and
turns her two rules into something that can live on an object.

## 4. Brad Wolfe, the builder who approves the agent's work

Post: one agent never gets both the idea and the execution button, the finance rule that preparer and
approver are different people, and the observation that the person who wrote the agent's matching
rules is usually the one signing off the matches. His test: run the segregation of duties report with
every agent listed as a user and its builder named beside it, because a clean report that does not
list the agents tested nothing.

Draft, paste as a comment.

---

The test is right and it usually fails one step before the report. In most organisations an agent cannot be listed as a user, because it has no identity to list. It runs under a service account that people also use, or under the builder's own credentials, so the action lands on a human who was not in the room and the conflict is invisible rather than unreported.

Giving the agent its own principal is cheap and it is a prerequisite, not a governance programme. Put the builder in the record beside every action the agent takes. Then the report you describe has a population to read, and the review that follows is a query rather than a series of interviews.

The line worth keeping is yours. A clean report over a population that excludes the agents did not test anything.

---

152 words. The addition is mechanical and checkable: an agent with no principal cannot appear in an
access review, so the control fails at the identity layer before the conflict layer. This is the third
time I have commented on his work in two days, which is one too many after this one.

## 5. Alex Banks, the blue collar rebound

Post: the FT chart on AI and jobs, output on trend while tech employment falls, the point that the
productivity jump started in 2020 and much of the current cutting is pandemic hiring being unwound,
then the physical world as the constraint. Electrical work at 45 to 70 percent of data centre build
cost from the IBEW, 349,000 construction workers needed in 2026 from the ABC, Meta spending 115
million dollars on trades training.

Draft, paste as a comment.

---

The direction holds and three of the figures want a source note before anyone plans against them. The 45 to 70 percent for electrical work comes from the IBEW, a union, and the 349,000 comes from Associated Builders and Contractors, a contractor association. Both are the best public numbers there are, and both are produced by organisations with a position on the answer.

That is not a reason to drop them. It is a reason to say whose model it is, because a workforce projection commissioned by the people who want more workers hired tends to be the one that argues for more hiring.

The employment line is the one I would test hardest. Whether it covers the whole sector or a fixed set of firms decides what the fall means, and the AI native companies hiring hardest now are not in a 2019 cohort.

---

151 words. Provenance rather than disagreement, which is what worked on the Blue Cross comment. It
stays inside what his own citations say, so nothing here is a claim about the FT that he cannot check.
