## Why four and not a list

Six questions before a call turns into homework, which he was promised he would not have. Four can be read in a minute and answered from memory, and the fourth is the one worth the hour.

## Why each carries an opinion

A bare question puts him in an exam. A question with a stated guess gives him something to agree with or knock down, which is what a man who has argued with specifications for fifty-five years actually enjoys.

## Why the fourth is last

It is the biggest question and the only one that is about him rather than about the protocol. Put last, it either ends the call or sets up the closing question already in the script, and it does not compete with the three that concern the work.
