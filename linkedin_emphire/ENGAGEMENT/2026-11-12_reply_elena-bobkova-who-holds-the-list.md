## Reply, paste as is

Then the two answers meet, which is probably why mine kept sliding toward yours. A subcontractor's access is the credential with no retirement event: it was minted for one integration, the person who used it moved off the project, and the review that would catch it happens inside a company that never employed them.

What makes it harder than the tooling is that the register is split. The party holding the credential and the party carrying the consequence are different organisations, and neither ends up with the full list, so nobody can say what can authenticate across that boundary.

The question I would carry forward is not which controls, but who is expected to hold that list. Supply chain risk gets audited as a questionnaire, and a questionnaire cannot count an identity.

## Why not simply concede

She was generous about the miss, so the reply does not need to defend the guess and does not pretend it was right. What it does instead is show that the two answers describe the same population from opposite ends, which is true and is more useful to her than agreement.

The consequence boundary is the part worth adding. Vendor risk is normally handled as assurance: questionnaires, attestations, contractual clauses. All of those measure whether a supplier says it has controls. None of them produces a count of identities that can authenticate into the estate, and that count is what her incidents turn on if the third party is where the credentials live.

Her comment closed with a thank you and no question, so the reply carries the idea and stops. Nothing is asked of her.

## If she replies

The follow-up worth having is contractual rather than technical: does any agreement she audits require the supplier to report identities retired and revoked, and who reconciles the two lists. One question, only if she opens the door.
