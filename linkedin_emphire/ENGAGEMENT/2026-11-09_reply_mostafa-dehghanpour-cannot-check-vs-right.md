## The angle

His two explanations leave different traces, so they can be separated rather than debated. Where people cannot check, review
time collapses and disagreement clusters on the items whose evidence was hardest to assemble. Where the answer is simply right,
review time stays normal and the challenging questions stop being asked at all.

Then the upstream cause: whether the reviewer sees the evidence the chain used or only a summary of it. A reviewer handed a
conclusion and a paragraph cannot produce a challenge no matter how much time the workflow allows, which makes the time
objection a design output more often than a personal shortcoming.

## Reply, paste as is

Both are worth separating, and the record usually can. If people cannot check, review time collapses and the disagreements cluster on the items whose evidence was hardest to assemble. If the answer really is right, review time stays normal and the challenging questions stop being asked at all.

What decides which one you get sits upstream: whether the reviewer sees the evidence the chain relied on, or only a summary of it. Somebody handed a conclusion and a paragraph cannot produce a challenge, however much time the workflow allows. So the time objection is often a design output rather than a personal one.

The test that separates them is a sample where a second person redoes the judgement from the source documents, blind to what the chain said. Agreement there tells you the answer was right. Disagreement tells you the reviewer could not have known.
