THE HUMAN GATE (10-24 fine-tune). The enemy is not
bad writing. The enemy is machine-polished
writing: perfect, agreeable, riskless,
forgettable. A reader (or the new AI slop
button) must be able to tell a human made this.
Calibration anchor: the 10-21 comment on the
Paolo Perrone thread (60,772 impressions, author
replied, peer celebrated). Its features are the
target: one specific angle taken from the post's
own points, one concession, one memorable line,
uneven rhythm, flat ending.

History note: the 10-23 gate said 0 contractions
after a contraction shipped live. The 10-24
fine-tune restores natural contractions because
zero-contraction copy, line after line, is
itself the tell: it reads machine-polished, and
Jada Balster's 10-24 post shows the market now
complain out loud about exactly that shape.

MECHANICAL GATE (fail any = do not publish,
search the text for each):
□ 0 em dashes in the text (owner banned, machine
  tell, absolute)
□ Contractions: 1-4 in a comment, placed where a
  human would (never in the first line, never two
  in one clause)
□ Straight quotes only, no curly quotes
□ No dense single block: one idea per breath
□ Paragraph lengths uneven (never three equal
  blocks; 2/1/2 sentences is the default shape
  for a three-paragraph comment)
□ Word budget by mode (COMMENT MODES below):
  OUTSTANDING 90-120, QUESTIONING 90-120,
  HUMAN 20-80, INTERACTIVE 15-60. Never longer
  than the post
□ No bullet points inside a comment (bullets
  belong to posts)
□ First line under 12 words, starts from a
  specific point in the post (a quoted phrase or
  a number), never a generic compliment (one
  specific, earned compliment allowed anywhere
  after line one, see COMPLIMENT RULE); no
  CATEGORY 7 openers or closers (banned_words.txt)
□ Ends on a flat statement or a concrete image;
  a question mark only in INTERACTIVE mode; no
  hashtags inside a comment
□ Structure rules PART 10.5 pass for the piece
  type: the signatures required by that type
  are present and the demoted devices D1-D3
  are absent (standalone: S1-S7 required;
  comment: S3 and S4 required where they fit,
  S7/S8 optional)

PART 10.5: STRUCTURE RULES (10-24 fine-tune,
owner: "the way the pasted posts and comments
are structured can finetune the engine; some
output still sounds generated, not human")
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

The words can pass the gate and the piece can
still read generated. The tell is not the
vocabulary, it is the shape: uniform paragraphs,
every sentence load-bearing, the point always
first, precision everywhere. The pastes were
mined (feed posts that read human, the owner's
own DMs and replies) and the owner's
VERIFIED_POSTS were read as ground truth.
Extend the owner's verified posts. Never copy
another writer's format: their numbered lists
and emoji numbering are not the goal, and the
owner's own posts have never used a list.

GROUND TRUTH SIGNATURES (from the owner's own
verified posts, the only format authority):
- 5-10 paragraphs of wildly uneven lengths
  (php2shell event post: 10; check-never-
  failed: 5; aithos: 6), not 3 equal blocks
- first-person habitual bridge: "I keep seeing
  this in the work." / "I see the same failure
  from the other side of the stack." / "Last
  week I tried to break a support chatbot I
  had spent a month building."
- one pivot line per post: "But here is the
  part nobody says out loud." / "And the honest
  part:" / "The discovery story is the other
  lesson."
- period fragments: "In the runtime." /
  "Before any application code runs." / "That
  is the only proof I have."
- echo couplets: "All of those look exactly
  the same from the dashboard. All of them are
  green." / "Calif found it with GPT-5.6 Sol.
  Wiz found it independently."
- plain words where the polished word would
  go: "a thing I know is bad" / "a
  conversation with whoever owns the server"
- hedged precision: one dense exact pile (the
  versions, the CVEs), everything else plain
  ("about 70 percent", "millions of attempts",
  "on a stock install")
- flat close, sometimes with a sardonic
  fragment that does not land: "Break it
  before your users do, or their relatives
  do."

S1. STANDALONE SHAPE: 5-8 paragraphs. At least
    one is a 2-6 word beat (a fragment, an
    imperative, or a colon-beat like "And the
    honest part:"). At least one is a dense
    paragraph (4+ sentences) when the piece
    carries facts. Three equal-length
    paragraphs is banned as a standalone shape
    (it was this week's default and it is the
    shape the owner names as generated).
S2. FIRST-PERSON HABITUAL BRIDGE (standalone
    required, comment allowed): one line
    where the writer states what he keeps
    seeing, doing, or finding out, as a habit,
    not a confession. It states the writer's
    relation to the topic; it does not restate
    the thesis.
S3. PERIOD FRAGMENTS: 1-3 in a standalone,
    0-1 in a comment. A 3-6 word fragment that
    ends a thought a full sentence would carry.
S4. PLAIN WORD (required, both types): one
    place where the plain word goes where the
    polished word would ("a thing", "on the
    wall", "went sideways"). A piece where
    every noun is precise reads generated.
S5. PRECISION SPREAD (pieces with facts): one
    dense exact pile (versions, CVEs, scores,
    dates); everything else hedged and plain
    ("about", "most of", "a few",
    "considerably more"). Never two dense
    piles, never all hedged.
S6. THE PIVOT (standalone required): the piece
    is two halves and one sentence is the
    hinge. The hinge must not reuse a pivot
    line from a post in the last 7 days (the
    owner's pivot lines are a finite set;
    reusing one is a motif violation).
S7. UNLANDED DETAIL (0-1, both types): one
    specific detail that serves the argument
    only a little, stated and left (a number,
    a name, a scene; the reader could check
    it; the argument does not need it). This
    is the human waste that generated text has
    none of. Never two.
S8. COMMA-CHAIN SENTENCE (0-1, both types):
    one sentence of 25+ words chained with
    commas, the point arriving in the middle
    or at the end (the owner's own DM
    structure: "this is a business deal not a
    personal favor"). Generated copy puts the
    point first, every time.
S9. PARENTHETICAL ASIDE (post 0-1): one
    parenthetical that trails off without
    landing (the Hamiel "especially after so
    much investment in cybersecurity from AI
    companies..."). Only when the joke is
    already in the piece's tone.

DEMOTED DEVICES (the engine's own overused
moves, now tells; search before publishing):
D1. "Not: X. This: Y." antithesis: max once
    per 3 posts (used in the Sam 58w reply,
    the Praveen 112w, and the first vulnzero
    draft on the same day; the device was
    becoming a motif).
D2. The triple "no X, no Y, no Z": max once
    per post.
D3. Four uniform paragraphs: banned as a
    standalone shape (see S1).

ARGUMENT TEST (the Jada Balster test):
Could a hostile reader find one line here to
argue with? A comment that flatters the post
from start to finish is a summary wearing a
compliment. Every comment must carry at least
one of:
- a claim that could be wrong, or
- a specific concession, or
- a named disagreement (one line, respectful), or
- a practitioner detail the post does not have
If nothing in the comment risks being wrong,
rewrite it or delete it.

SELF-REFERENCE RULE:
At least one of every three comments contains a
self-reference that traces to the archive
(VERIFIED_POSTS, published_comments,
performance_tracker). No invented incidents. If
there is no traceable fact, use a generic craft
line, not a made-up story.

COMPLIMENT RULE (10-24b, owner direction: some
comments should carry the compliment that best
fits):
- A specific, earned compliment is allowed in
  one place in a comment, never as the first
  line. It points at a particular line, number,
  or choice in the post, and says why it works
  (patterns: "the [line] is the one doing the
  work here", "the [number] is the part I'd
  take from this"). The author must be able to
  point at the exact line you mean.
- If the compliment could sit on top of any
  post, it is generic, and CATEGORY 7 applies:
  cut it.
- By mode: OUTSTANDING allowed (position two,
  right after the hook) when the post has a
  line worth naming. INTERACTIVE allowed and
  usually right: acknowledge what they said
  before adding. HUMAN required: the specific
  compliment is the payload, name the moment
  and why it matters. QUESTIONING optional:
  at most one short acknowledgment, dropped
  entirely if the challenge needs the words.
- One compliment per comment. A second one
  turns the comment into a tribute.

No two comments in the same 24h reuse the same
closing line (kept from 10-23).

VOICE QUESTIONS (all must be yes):
□ Does this sound like Timothy wrote it?
□ Would I actually say these words out loud?
□ Does it include vulnerability or honesty?
□ Does cultural voice feel authentic, not forced?
□ Does this add value to my audience?
□ Would my mentees recognize this as me?
□ If it is a comment: does the ending differ from
  the last three comments (not every comment is a
  question, and one line must be memorable)?

If any answer is "no" — revise before publishing.

╔═══════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║   COMMENT MODES (10-24: pick before drafting)    ║
╚═══════════════════════════════════════════════════╝

Pick the mode from the surface, then draft
inside its budget. One mode per comment.

OUTSTANDING (tier 1-2 surface, main comment):
90-120 words. One memorable line allowed: a
line quotable on its own, that would survive
being posted standalone. Full risk: the
argument test passes with a claim or a
concession, not with a question. One specific
compliment allowed in position two (the line
worth naming), never more.

INTERACTIVE (reply to an author, a thread
partner, or a commenter on the owner's post):
15-60 words. Direct address. Answer what was
said before adding anything. A specific
compliment to their point is allowed and
usually right here. A question mark is allowed
here, and only here, and only when the thread
needs the next move. Same day, first-class: a
reply is not an afterthought.

QUESTIONING (post contains a checkable claim,
number, or prediction):
90-120 words. Lead with the specific line that
does not hold. Serve one re-runnable counter
or name the missing check. If the macro claim
cannot be verified from here, do not argue the
macro claim, argue the checkable part. No
snark, no "I disagree" opener, one concrete
example. Compliment optional: at most one
short acknowledgment, dropped if the challenge
needs the words.

HUMAN (small, personal, community, celebration
posts):
20-80 words. Warm and specific to the moment
(name the person's moment, never a generic
"great journey"). The specific compliment is
the payload here: name the moment and why it
matters, per the COMPLIMENT RULE. Frame
invisible: do not drop the check theorem
unless the post is literally about it. No
attempt at a memorable line. End flat.

MODE BALANCE (per day):
- Max 2 OUTSTANDING per day.
- If the day's surfaces include a checkable
  claim, at least one goes QUESTIONING.
- Personal and community posts get HUMAN, not
  HUMAN-shaped OUTSTANDING.
- INTERACTIVE replies count against the daily
  action cap (8 base, 12 max), not against the
  OUTSTANDING cap.
