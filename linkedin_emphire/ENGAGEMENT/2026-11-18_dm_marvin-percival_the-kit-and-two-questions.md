## Message, paste as is

<!-- gate:body:start -->
Marvin, here is the test case I would start with, and it is smaller than the first one I had in mind.

Every open protocol dies in the same place. An adopter cannot tell whether their own implementation follows the spec, so they ask the author, and the author is one person. My review took ten days and it was one reviewer for one implementation. That does not scale past the second customer.

So the thing to build is the kit. A runner an adopter points at their own deployment, thirty checks taken straight from numbered rules in the specification, a verdict per check with the rule printed beside it, and a deliberately broken implementation the kit has to fail so it proves it can fail.

Mine to build, yours to review, both names on it, published under the protocol. The rules and the numbering stay yours because only you know what each one means. I write the checks and the report, and the report says what the kit does not check, which is the line that makes it worth anything.

If the first ten checks turn up a rule that cannot be written as something observable, that is a finding and it goes back into the specification.

Two things I keep coming back to. Has anybody paid for FORAY yet, and for what? And is there one of the twenty-four with a named buyer, because that is the one worth taking to market first and the rest can wait.

The clinical trial idea still stands. I would rather earn the kit first.

## Why the kit and not the product

The clinical product needs a buyer the owner would have to find alone in a market he is still learning. The kit is
needed by the first serious adopter of a protocol that already exists, it can be built in four weeks, and it produces
a public artefact with his name beside it. It is also the cheapest possible way to find out how he works.

## Why the two market questions are asked and the third is not

Asking whether anybody has paid, and which project has a named buyer, are questions a builder answers without
embarrassment and they produce information that decides the next six months. Asking whether there is a place in his
team, in writing, forces a polite answer before either side has seen the other work.

## Why the last line gives something up

Naming the clinical idea as the second case, and saying the kit has to be earned first, is the sentence that stops the
message reading as a person collecting opportunities.
