# The buyer, checked against the record

What this is: the buyer and the selling point, built from who actually answered rather than from who should. Who it is for:
the owner, before the lead list is built. What to do next: agree or correct the buyer in section 3, because the lead list in
the next round is built to it.

## 1. What the book says to do first, and why it has not been done

Sell Like Crazy, Phase 1: find the **Power 4%**, the customers who pay most, and build the buyer from them. Not from
imagination, from the people who already paid.

There's one payment in the record: Sudhir, 90 dollars, for tutoring. So the eight earlier ICP documents were each built from
what a buyer ought to be, and nobody in them has answered since.

There's a better dataset than payments, though, and it's already in the repo: **every person who answered, engaged and
got as far as a price or a scope.** That is what this note is built on.

## 3. The buyer, verified

**Who:** the founder or CTO of a 5 to 50 person company selling an AI agent, a guardrail, a governance or an evidence
product, whose product makes a claim about safety or control.

**When:** the week a named customer, an investor or an auditor asks them for independent evidence of that claim. A pilot
acceptance, an enterprise security questionnaire, a procurement review, due diligence for a round.

**Why this one and not the others:** seven of them have already answered, so the first half is proven. What every one of
the seven lacked is the trigger, and that's what turns "I value this" into a budget, because the
money then comes out of the deal they are trying to close.

**What they say, in their words, from the threads:** "what would you try to break first", "is this a mutual proof of concept
or a paid engagement", "self-funded, no budget for external audits", "I want to see what you do".

**What they fear:** the customer's security reviewer asks one question they cannot answer, and the deal slips a quarter.

**What they want:** a document they can forward to the buyer that the buyer will believe, because someone outside the
company wrote it and every finding can be rerun.

**Who it is not, on the evidence:**
- A CISO at a 20 to 500 person company, the earlier ICP-A. Zero conversations in the record.
- Career changers and students, 17 percent of arrivals in the 13 November reading. They read and never buy.
- Nigerian banks and fintechs, the October tier 1. Zero conversations in the record. Not dead, just unproven.
- Pre-revenue builders with no trigger. They are the referral and reputation lane, not the revenue lane.

## 4. The selling point, verified

Checked against what people actually reacted to, not what the profile says:

| What happened | What it shows they value |
| --- | --- |
| Marvin cleared a case study that lists its own seven corrections | a review that names where it was wrong |
| Behar reran every pack himself from the hashes and passed it | evidence a stranger can rerun without you |
| Travis corrected the five-million figure and was thanked for it | claims that come with their source |
| Kuldeep's first question was what you would break first | specific attacks, not a framework |
| The Nvidia post reached 8,163; "a control only its owner can invoke" | evidence readable from outside |

**The selling point, one line:** *Independent evidence your customer will accept: every finding reruns from what I hand
you, and the report says what was not tested*.

**Why it is hard to copy:** almost nobody in this market sends a "what was not tested" page, almost nobody makes findings
rerunnable by the buyer, and the owner has two named people (Marvin, Behar) who will say both are true.

**What it replaces:** "I break AI systems before your users do" stays as the headline, because it's what a follower
remembers. It's the wrong line for the sale. This buyer isn't afraid of users; they're afraid of their own customer's
reviewer.

## 5. Money left on the table, from the book

In order of cost.

1. **Free service instead of a free document.** The book's free thing is the bait (Phase 2): a report, a checklist, a video.
   The service itself is always priced (Phase 4). The record has four free engagements (Arcezia, FORAY, Behar, Martina's
   offer on 18 November), together several weeks of work. Give away the playbook, and put a price on the work, even
   a small one.
2. **Nothing asked for in return for the free work.** Every free engagement ended with thanks and no introduction. The book
   is blunt that the value given first is an investment, and an investment has a return. Ask for one introduction each: "who do you know whose customer is asking for evidence?" Travis sells to buyers for a living. Marvin
   and Behar both offered to vouch.
3. **No diagnosis before the price** (Phase 7, sell like a doctor). Kuldeep got a price on the day he asked. Three days later
   he said there was no budget. Two questions would have found it first: "who is asking you for this evidence?"
   and "by when?" No asker and no date means no budget, whatever the interest.
4. **No nurture after a no** (Phase 6). Kuldeep and Sébastien both said no with the door open, and neither has a follow-up
   date. Both will have a first customer one day, and that is the day they need the evidence. A date in the board, and one
   useful message a month, costs nothing.
5. **No offer, only a price list** (Phase 4). The 13 November note wrote the guarantee, the scarcity line, the premium stack
   and the retainer. On 15 November none were live: the 1 dollar re-test add-on was still on the listing, there was no
   guarantee, no retainer, and 0 proposals had gone out in seven days. It was written and never applied.
6. **The bait has no hook** (Phase 3). The 168-class playbook is public and nobody has to ask for it, so nobody does. The
   first-comment "comment checklist and I will send it filled for your system" line was written on 13 November and has not
   appeared under a post since.
7. **No back end** (Phase 8). Every engagement is one invoice. The monthly re-test block is still not a product.

## 6. What changes, for the lead list in the next round

- Build the list to section 3: founders and CTOs of AI agent, guardrail, governance and evidence companies, 5 to 50
  people, **with a dated public trigger**: a pilot or partnership announced, a security or compliance page published, a
  round raised, a SOC 2 or ISO 42001 process announced, an enterprise customer named.
- Each lead carries the trigger, its date and where it was seen, so a stranger can check it. A lead with no trigger is not a
  lead.
- Every first message asks the two diagnosis questions before naming a price.
- Leave the seven people in section 2 off the list. They are the referral lane, and each gets one introduction ask.

## 7. How this could be wrong

Eight people is a small sample, and none of them paid, so the trigger is a hypothesis. It is tested by the list: if 20
founders with a dated trigger produce no conversation that reaches a price within three weeks, the trigger is wrong, and
the next candidate is the vendor's customer instead of the vendor, the enterprise buyer running the review.
