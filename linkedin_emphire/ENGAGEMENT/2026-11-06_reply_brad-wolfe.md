# Reply to Brad Wolfe, 6 November 2026

His comment, in three parts: most finance stacks hold only the current definition; the reporting layer
recalculates history each run; finance already has the fix for a published number, which is a
restatement note showing old figure, new figure, difference, and for definitions, running old and new
side by side for one period, freezing both, and carrying a signed "definition change" line.

He is right, and the reply should not congratulate him for it. What he cannot see from the CFO chair
is that a note is a control that depends on a person remembering, and that in AI product metrics the
change rate is high enough to break the accounting analogy. Both points are his to use, not mine to
score.

## Draft, paste as a reply

Agreed on the restatement note, and the side by side period is the part most teams skip.

The gap is that a note is a control that depends on somebody remembering. If the definition version
sits on the fact row as a key rather than a label on a dashboard, the note stops being a document and
becomes a diff. Run both versions over the same frozen window and the line item falls out of the
comparison instead of being written by hand.

That matters more on your side of the table than mine. Audit committees accept exceptions, not
streams. Restatement works in accounting because changes are rare and reviewed. AI product metrics get
redefined every few sprints, so if every change needs a human note, the note becomes noise and the
discipline dies quietly. Generate it or lose it.

And the capital decision inherits all of it. The ROI you underwrite is only as durable as the
definition of the number you underwrote it with. If the definition moves and nobody restates, the
investment case restates itself in the board pack with nothing on the page to say so.

Version the definition like a schema and the board pack gets its line for free.
