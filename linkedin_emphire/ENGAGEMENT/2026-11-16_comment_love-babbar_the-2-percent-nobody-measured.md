## Comment, paste as is

The 56% is the number I'd want the invoice for. The 2% is the number I'd want the test for.

A saving is measured to the cent and it shows up in a dashboard the same week. A quality loss is measured by whatever suite happens to be standing, and that suite is often one nobody has ever asked to fail. One verifier I audited compared 16 of 64 hash characters, passed fifty checks, and could not have failed if the file were wrong. A 2% dip walks straight through that and the invoice still looks like a triumph.

That's the half of the discipline AT&amp;T's numbers can't show. Routing 40% of traffic to the cheap model is a decision you can only bank if someone can prove the dip is 2% and not 6%. Otherwise you've booked a saving and bought a decay on credit.

Your last point is the sharpest thing in the post. Fifty-six percent off reads as winning. Two percent worse reads as losing. Same trade, and the label does all the deciding.

## Why this and not the routing argument

Every reply under a post like this will be about model selection, because that is what the bullet points are. The comment goes to the sentence he flagged himself, the 2% versus half the cost, and adds the thing his list cannot contain: a saving has an instrument and a loss has an opinion.

That is also the only place this account has standing. The verifier that compared 16 of 64 characters is a published finding, so the claim in the third paragraph is evidence rather than an anecdote about somewhere else.
