# Comment on the unnamed After Visibility series post, 7 November 2026

## What the post claims

95.7 per cent of brands are recognised by AI at the start of a buying conversation and 87.3 per cent are handed to
a competitor by the fourth question. Nine parts follow, twice a week to 3 December, and the call to action is a
newsletter subscribe.

Two things are true at once here. The distinction the series is built on, recognition at the door versus selection
inside the conversation, is real and is not widely measured. And the numbers are precise to a tenth of a per cent
while the underlying research is not published in the post, so nothing about them can be checked from the outside.

## Draft, paste as a comment

"Handed to a competitor by the fourth question" is a strong claim, and the thing I would want to see behind it is
the reason for the drop. An agent that rules you out on a stated constraint and one that never retrieved you at all
look the same in the outcome, and only the first tells you what to fix.

If the research records which of the two happened, that is worth more than the percentage. If it does not, the gap
is measured but not attributed, and the next eight parts inherit it.

## Why this shape

- Does not dispute the numbers. Arguing with an unpublished dataset in a comment is unwinnable and looks like
  defensiveness from someone with no data of their own in the thread.
- Asks the one question that separates a measurement from a marketing series, in the writer's own vocabulary. A
  writer who has the data will answer it; one who does not will change the subject.
- Short on purpose. Under a launch post the comment is read by people deciding whether to subscribe, and a hundred
  words is the most they will read.

## Posting notes, and one reservation

- Do not post this until the author is known. Every other deliverable this week had a profile read behind it and this
  one does not.
- The reservation worth stating plainly: a funnel post that leads with figures precise to a decimal place, sourced
  only to "our own research", is exactly the shape your own rules treat as unverifiable. A comment under it lends a
  technical name to numbers nobody outside the newsletter can check. If the author turns out to be a practitioner
  publishing the dataset, the comment is right. If not, the better move is to say nothing here and put the same idea
  into your own post, where you control the claim.
