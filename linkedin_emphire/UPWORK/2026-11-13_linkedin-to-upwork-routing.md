# Moving LinkedIn readers to Upwork

The short answer: yes, and not the way it is usually done. A "read more" link in the post body is the one route that costs
reach and returns almost nothing, and it is also the route your own rule already forbids.

## What is and is not allowed

| Direction | Status | Why |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn post or profile, to your Upwork profile or catalog | Allowed | Upwork's own catalog page says it: share a link to anyone, including on your social feeds. This is the direction you are asking about. |
| Upwork proposal or message, to your LinkedIn, email, or WhatsApp | Not allowed | Contact details in a message read as circumvention, and asking once is enough. This is the direction that gets accounts banned. |
| A client you met on LinkedIn, hired through Upwork | Allowed, and worth it for the first few | It starts off platform, so it stays yours. You get escrow and a review, and Upwork gets 10 percent. |
| A client you met on Upwork, taken off platform inside two years | Not allowed | The two-year rule runs from first contact. Upwork's help pages list an off-platform origin as an exception, which is exactly why the LinkedIn-sourced client is the safe one to route. |

The asymmetry is the whole strategy. Bring people in from LinkedIn freely, never send anyone out from Upwork.

## Upwork has no blog

There is no article editor and no hosted long-form page. What exists, and what each can carry:

- **Portfolio items.** A title, a description of a few paragraphs, images, and attachments. This is the closest thing to a
  case study page, and it is where a reader who is already curious will go.
- **Project Catalog listings.** A description, three tiers, and a delivery time. This is a shop page, not reading.
- **Community posts.** A forum thread on Upwork's own domain. It can hold a long post and it is public, but it converts
  to nothing directly and it lives under Upwork's brand rather than yours.

So the article you are imagining has to live on LinkedIn, where the writing already happens, and Upwork has to be the
**checkout** it points at. That is a different thing from a "read more", and it converts better.

## Why the read-more link is the worst version

Three reasons, in order of cost:

1. **It costs reach.** LinkedIn suppresses posts that send people off platform. A post carrying an external link reaches a
   fraction of the same post without one, and you are building from 1,148 followers, so the fraction matters.
2. **It breaks your own rule.** No link, no pitch, no hashtag beyond the four, in the body of a post. The place for a link
   is the first comment, and that slot is held by the playbook until it has a thousand stars.
3. **The arithmetic is not there.** 572 profile views and 9,192 impressions in the last period, 5 and 6 catalog views in
   thirty days, and 0 orders. A one percent click-through on 572 views is five people, and at that volume the bottleneck
   was never traffic. It is that nobody who arrived had decided to buy.

Diversion is a closing move, not an acquisition move. It works on the person who has already asked what it costs.

## The three routes that do work, in order

### 1. The Featured section of the LinkedIn profile

Every profile view lands here, and it is permanent. `572 profile views` is the number to look at, not impressions,
because a profile view is someone checking whether you are real.

Add three entries. The URLs below are the live pages, opened on 13 November 2026 and checked against what they actually
sell.

| Entry title | URL | What the page is |
|---|---|---|
| AI red team test, priced by depth | `https://www.upwork.com/services/product/development-it-an-ai-red-team-test-and-prompt-injection-audit-2109166740052666541` | $150 scan in 3 days, $600 four-layer test in 7, $1,500 test plus scoped fixes in 14. The only page with a price on it. |
| Review my AI design before I build it | `https://www.upwork.com/services/product/development-it-timothy-1697327656243625984` | The paid consultation: a video call, a prioritised risk list, a time and cost estimate, and a written summary. |
| Build it with the testing inside | `https://www.upwork.com/services/product/development-it-an-ai-chatbot-or-agent-built-tested-and-hardened-2109189706859183826` | Chatbot or agent build at $300, $900 and $2,500, with the regression suite included rather than sold after. |

Order them as above: price first, then the cheap first step, then the bigger build. The profile link itself is
`https://www.upwork.com/freelancers/timothyo28`, and it is worth putting in the About section as plain text once, since a
Featured card can break but a URL cannot.

The case study does not belong in Featured as a third Upwork card. It is the proof of judgement, it lives on LinkedIn
already, and a fourth card dilutes the three that lead to a checkout.

### 1c. The section as it stands, and the order it should be in

On 13 November the section held four cards, in this order: the playbook repo, the My Mission carousel, the case study
carousel, and a DM Me card. That is a portfolio sequence: it answers "who is this person" and never answers "what does
this cost and how do I start". A stranger who lands on the profile sees an engineer first and a shop second, and the shop
is where the money is.

Six cards is too many for the two that are visible on a phone; five is enough, and the order is the whole message:

| Slot | Card | Added with | Why it is here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | AI red team test, priced by depth | Add a link | The only card that states a price and a delivery date. It is the first thing a stranger should be able to buy. |
| 2 | Review my AI design before I build it | Add a link | The cheap first yes, directly under the priced project, so someone who is not ready for a build still has a button. |
| 3 | Teamthy/Ai-Red-Team-Playbook | already there, move down | Credibility, and the one card that proves the work is public. It answers the doubt created by slots one and two. |
| 4 | Case Study: data to deployment | already there, move down | Proof of judgement for the people shopping for a full build rather than a test. |
| 5 | Build it with the testing inside | Add a link | The $300 to $2,500 build. Fifth because a build is the slowest and rarest sale, and it is here for the reader who scrolls. |

**Delete the DM Me card.** It spends a slot to say "message me" and gives the reader nothing to click toward. The post
itself can stay in the feed; the slot should belong to a page that takes money.

**Move My Mission to the end, or drop it.** It points at the About section, which is already visible on the same page one
scroll below. A Featured card that duplicates the page it sits on is the cheapest kind of wasted slot.

### 1d. Paste copy for the three link cards

The Add a link dialog gives you a URL, a Title, a Description and a Thumbnail. The Description is the only field most
people leave empty, and it is the only field that can carry a price before the click.

**Card 1. Title:** AI red team test, priced by depth
Description (179 characters):
```
A 15-attack injection scan from $150 in 3 days, a four-layer test at $600 in 7, or the test plus scoped fixes at $1,500. You get the attack, the evidence and the fix, not a score.
```

**Card 2. Title:** Review my AI design before I build it
Description (189 characters):
```
45 minutes on your AI feature, API or agent: where it can be attacked, what it costs to close, what to do first. You leave with a prioritised list, an effort estimate and a written summary.
```

**Card 3. Title:** Build it with the testing inside
Description (169 characters):
```
A chatbot or agent built in Go, Node or Python at $300, $900 or $2,500, with the regression suite written during the build so the second version does not undo the first.
```

Keep the thumbnail Upwork offers. The one on card 1 reads "15-Point Scan. Know Before You Spend" over a three-day promise, which is the right face for the section: it looks like a product and it names a number.

Mechanics, because the menu offers four ways to get this wrong:

1. Click **+**, then **Add a link**. Not Add a post, which pulls an existing post and cannot point anywhere new. Not Add a
   document, which uploads a file and renders a carousel. Not Add an image, which cannot carry a destination.
2. Paste the full URL, wait for the preview card to fill in. On these pages the card title comes from Upwork's own page
   title, so it may read "You will get an AI red team test and prompt injection audit". Where LinkedIn offers a title
   field, shorten it to the text in the table above.
3. If the preview comes back blank, keep it. A clickable card with a grey box still costs nothing and still links to a
   page that shows a price; delete it only if it fails to link.
4. Reorder with the handles on the Reorder screen. The order is not cosmetic: two cards sit above the fold on mobile, so
   slots one and two are the entire first impression for anyone arriving from a comment.

### 1b. The consultation is the first-review machine, and it is mispriced if it is priced low

Of the three pages, the consultation is the only one that can produce a completed contract with no build and no scoping
call: a client books, pays, turns up, and the contract closes. That is the shortest path to the review and the Job Success
Score that the whole account is missing.

Two consequences:

1. **Price it at $75 to $95 for 45 minutes**, not lower. Under about $50 it reads as a free call with a payment step, and
   the client who books a free call does not leave a review.
2. **Ask for the review at the end of the call, in plain words.** The consultation is the one product where the client has
   already received everything by the time you ask.

That single contract is worth more than three $150 scans, because it moves the profile from no score to scored, and every
proposal after it argues from a 5.0 instead of from nothing.

### 2. The DM handoff, at the moment price is asked

This is where money is won or lost, and the message is three lines. Do not send it before someone asks.

```
Two ways to do this. A fixed-scope review at a set price, which is the catalog listing here:
https://www.upwork.com/services/product/development-it-an-ai-red-team-test-and-prompt-injection-audit-2109166740052666541
with escrow and a delivery date, or an invoice on delivery if you would rather keep it simple and you are not already on
Upwork.

If you tell me what the system does and what it is allowed to touch, I will tell you which of those fits.
```

If the person is earlier than that, unsure what they even need, the consultation link is the better send, because it asks
for an hour rather than a project.

The link is allowed because the conversation started on LinkedIn. The same link inside an Upwork message would not be.

### 3. The portfolio item that ends in an offer

Every portfolio item currently ends in proof. Two of them should end in a price, one sentence, no sales voice:

> The four-layer test this report comes from is the Standard tier of my project catalog, $600, delivered in seven days.

That sentence is the difference between a reader who admires the report and a reader who clicks the listing. Put it in
item 3 (findings report) and item 2 (red team lab), and leave items 1, 4, 5 and 6 as proof.

## What to write on LinkedIn instead of a read-more

If the goal is to move writing to where it does two jobs at once, the form is:

1. **A post that stands alone.** No link, no teaser, nothing withheld. This is why the existing posts work.
2. **The link in the first comment** once the playbook slot frees up, and in the Featured section from today.
3. **A LinkedIn article for the long version**, because articles can carry links without the post penalty and they are
   indexed outside your follower graph. The article is the "blog" you were describing, with Upwork at the end of it.

Sequence for a given piece of work: post on LinkedIn (the argument), article on LinkedIn (the long version, ending in the
catalog link), portfolio item on Upwork (the artefact), catalog listing (the price). One artefact, four surfaces, and only
two of them are on the same platform.

## What to measure, and what would prove this wrong

- **Catalog views**, which you can already see per listing in the catalog dashboard: 5 and 6 in the last thirty days.
- **Profile views on LinkedIn**: 572 in the last period, from the analytics export.
- **DMs that ask a price**, counted by hand, because that is the only number that predicts money.

Read it in two weeks. If profile views hold and catalog views rise without orders, the bottleneck is the listing page and
not the route, and the fix is the offer, the price or the first screen of the description. If catalog views do not move at
all, the Featured section is not being seen and the route needs to be the DM instead.

## What not to do

- No "read more on Upwork" in a post body, and no link of any kind there.
- No LinkedIn URL inside an Upwork proposal or message.
- No new Upwork listing for every post. Two catalog listings and six portfolio items is already more than a buyer reads.
- No article that is a repost of a LinkedIn post with a different first line. That is the pattern that reads as generated,
  and it is the one thing the whole system is built to avoid.
