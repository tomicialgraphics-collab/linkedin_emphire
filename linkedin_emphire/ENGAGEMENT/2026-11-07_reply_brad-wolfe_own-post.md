# Reply to Brad Wolfe on the ten-run post, 7 November 2026

## What he said

Three points, all usable. The two tests stack, because the rule the agent is supposed to check against is the
same rule nobody in the building can state. The refusal point is one finance already knows: auditors do not
only sample the payments that went out, they ask for the ones that were blocked, who blocked them and under
which limit, because a system that records only successes cannot show a control ever operated. And the ten-run
sample should be drawn from runs that touched money or customer data, not from all runs.

The second one is better than anything in the post. It converts the refusal record from a good practice into a
requirement, and it does it from the audit side rather than from mine.

## Draft, paste as a reply

Brad, the audit point is the one I would build on. Sampling blocked payments is how a control gets proved to
have operated, and most agent stacks cannot produce that population at all, because the refusal never became
a row.

Money and customer data first makes sense. I would add reversibility as the second axis. A meeting summary can
be wrong and corrected. A payment release cannot be undone by editing a sentence. At least three of the ten
should be actions that cannot be taken back.

Documents first, agreed, and for a reason worth stating: a rule nobody in the building can state is a rule no
agent can check against.

## Why this shape

- Takes his audit example and says why it is the strongest of the three, which is the sort of reply that keeps
  a thread alive without flattering anyone.
- Adds one axis rather than restating his. Reversibility is separable from sensitivity, and a sample weighted
  only by data class will still under-weight the irreversible action.
- Concedes the documents-first point and gives the reason in one clause, so the agreement carries information.
- Four sentences in the last paragraph would have diluted it. Sixty words shorter than the comment it answers.
