# Comment on Stephen Klein's post on the AI selloff, 6 November 2026

## What the record says

The selloff happened. 5 November 2026: Nasdaq closed 5.52 per cent lower, its worst day in nineteen
months, the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell more than 12 per cent, and roughly 2.3 trillion came
off the Nasdaq 100 with about 3 trillion off the S and P 500. Klein's figures hold.

The trigger reported by Reuters, Fox Business and Yahoo Finance is the midterm result and the
regulatory fear that followed, particularly around data centre development. Reuters does not put the
ROI question first. Klein does, and his argument is that the market has finally started asking it.

Both can be true, but they are not the same question, and a comment that simply agrees with him would
be repeating a framing rather than testing it. That separation is the comment, and it is also the only
thing here that is in lane.

## Draft, paste as a comment

The ROI question is right. The trigger is worth separating from it.

Most of Thursday's tape is being read as the midterm result and the regulatory fear around data
centres, with the capex worry riding on top. Those are different questions. A policy shock says
something about the path to revenue. It says nothing about whether the returns already booked are
real. Letting one answer for the other is how a sector gets written off and over-funded in the same
quarter.

The economics question stands on its own though, and the reason it goes unanswered is attribution. The
spend is centralised and visible. The savings are distributed: fewer escalations, shorter cycle times,
one person doing what took three. Each lands in a budget that never saw the invoice, so when the
board asks for the return, the only number in the room is the cost.

Ask for the baseline, not the story.
