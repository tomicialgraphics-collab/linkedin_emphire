## What happened

A stranger dropped an instruction into content he was reading, on a post he wrote about AI, in front of 3,012 impressions worth of audience. It is a joke, and it is also the exact mechanism he tests for a living. The reply should not be defensive and should not pretend the joke is a security incident. It should answer it, then use it.

## Reply, paste as is

Two hundred grams of flour, two eggs, three hundred millilitres of milk, a pinch of salt, and twenty minutes of rest before the pan. I am a person, so I can choose to comply, which is the only reason that instruction worked.

The interesting part is what just happened. A stranger put an instruction into content I was reading, and it drew on the same machinery that answers questions. Every agent taking instructions from a document, a ticket or a web page is running this test in production, all day, with nobody watching.

The defence is not a better prompt. It is that content cannot issue instructions, and whoever is speaking has to stay distinguishable from whatever is being read.

## Why comply first

Refusing would look like a bot failing the Turing test, and deflecting would look like a consultant protecting a brand. Doing the harmless thing and then explaining it is the only version where the reader learns something and nobody is embarrassed.

## What not to say

No lecture, no "as an AI", no mention of prompt injection by name before the third sentence, and no implication that he should have known better. He made a good joke on a good post and it gets a good answer.

This exchange is a ready-made post for later: a real injection attempt in the comments of a post about AI, answered by a human, on a thread where the same failure has cost companies money in production. Not this week.
