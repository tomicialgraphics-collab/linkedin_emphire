The rule is two substantive exchanges per thread, and this is the second. He agreed, so the only useful

He said refusal and escalation should be built into the operating model. Escalation is the weaker half
of that sentence, and almost every design gets it wrong in the same way. It is treated as the safe
default, and it becomes the quiet one: the agent raises a request for approval, the queue is unattended,
and the workflow advances because nobody said no. That is an allow path wearing a control's clothes.

## What it adds

Three questions that turn escalation from a diagram into a control: who receives it, within what time,
and what happens if nobody answers. The third is the one that decides whether the brake is real, and
default deny is the answer that survives a busy week. It is also the answer most likely to be argued
down in design review precisely because it stops things, which is worth saying to somebody whose job is
commercial growth.

The last paragraph is the cheap test: can you show afterwards that the refusal happened and which rule
fired. That is the difference between a control and a claim about a control, and it is the same order of
statement as a risk appetite, so it belongs in the same paragraph of the operating model.

## Reply, paste this

You've put your finger on the part most programmes skip. Escalation looks like the safe option and
usually becomes the quiet one: the agent asks for approval, nobody is watching, and the workflow
proceeds on the default.

So escalation needs the same treatment as the action it is stopping. Who receives it, within what time,
and what happens if nobody answers. If the answer to the last one is that the action goes ahead, the
brake is a notification with better branding.

Default deny is the only version that survives a busy week, and it is the one that gets argued down in
design review because it is the one that stops things.

The second property is cheap to test and rarely tested: afterwards, can you show that the refusal
happened and which rule fired. A control nobody can reproduce is a claim about a control. That belongs
in the operating model next to the risk appetite, because it is the same kind of statement.
