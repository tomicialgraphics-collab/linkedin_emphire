One constraint belongs in the record now, because it narrows the search without deciding anything. Both archives hash identically on both sides. Two identical archives cannot contain different members, byte for byte. If the version three archive in your hands and the one in mine are the same bytes, then the result file inside them is the same file, and hashing it cannot produce two different values. So the four mismatches have to be one of three things. Either the four files hashed are not those members, or the bytes changed between extraction and hashing, or the four values came from something other than these archives.

Your own sentence is the most useful line in the message. The comparison did not involve running the hashing again. If those four values were produced during the earlier examination, they may describe the files that examination handled rather than the files inside these archives. That would be a path association finding, and it would say nothing about the archive itself.

The second candidate is a text transform between extraction and hashing, which is common enough to rule out first. Four mismatches out of four is information in itself. A faulty extraction usually hits some members and not others. A transform applied by a tool, or values carried over from an earlier examination, hits all four together.
