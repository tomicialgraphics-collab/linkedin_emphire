## Comment, paste as is

The day test is the right instrument with one wrong column. Crossing out what AI can already do leaves you the work you'd like to keep, because the model's capability and your employer's permission are different lists.

Most of what fills a week isn't undone by what the model can't do. It's undone by who signs it off. In the jobs I've looked at closely, what resists is the part where one named person is wrong if the output is wrong, and that holds whatever the next model can do.

Which gives a better question to sit under the paragraph: when the AI's work is wrong and nobody catches it, whose Tuesday gets worse? Whatever the answer names tends to still be a job in 2035, and it's usually not the part anyone enjoys most.

The good version stays blurry because people describe ingredients as if the list were the recipe. One bottom-up Tuesday from somebody who actually does the work is the closest thing to a recipe I've seen suggested.

## Why the column and not the question

His four questions are good and none of them will be answered in a comment, so answering one would be noise. The part of the post that is actually actionable is the exercise at the end, and the exercise has a defect that only shows up when you try it on a real week.

Crossing out what a model can do measures capability. What fills a working week is largely determined by authority: which decisions a person is allowed to make, which documents need a signature, whose name goes on the approval. Those two lists overlap and they are not the same list, so the crossing-out method quietly returns a list of tasks that are enjoyable rather than a list of tasks that will still be paid for.

## The question it replaces

Asking who is wrong when the output is wrong is the smallest useful version of the accountability question, and it survives every capability improvement, because it is not a question about capability. It also has the property his four questions lack: a person can answer it about their own job this week, without predicting 2035.

That is the whole argument. The exercise is worth doing and it needs a different filter, one that points at the signature rather than at the skill.

## What this is not

No link, no offer, no mention of engagements. His post links his own newsletter, and a cold comment that adds a second link splits the traffic he is trying to earn.

Not a claim that the enjoyed work disappears, or that the accountable work is the good work. It is often the dullest part of the week, which is exactly why the crossing-out method skips it and why the method flatters whoever tries it.

## If he replies

The question worth having is what happens when the named person stops being able to check the work, because the volume outruns them. That is the failure mode of every accountability answer: the signature stays and the review behind it becomes a formality, which is how a job survives on paper and stops being one in practice.
