The integrity position, stated plainly

The archive digest e34c35c8a9269dddfd0ec9452b5a3b3e5894246362ce031c36be3d5b26b33f98 could not be checked, and the reason is structural rather than an omission by either side.

The 42 files that arrived at ec6e46f are the archive's contents, so the working set is complete. A zip's SHA-256 hashes the container, not the contents: the compression stream, the headers, the timestamps and the file order. Extraction discards all of that, so the digest cannot be recomputed from the files and it cannot be verified against them. A fresh re-zip would not match either, because the timestamps differ.

So the position is: content complete and pinned by manifest, container digest unverified. The reproduction below ran against those 42 files and against 006d7ee extracted from git, so the results do not depend on the container being the one that was hashed.

To convert that into the stronger claim, one of two things is needed. Either the manifest is checked on the sender's extracted copy, forty-two hashes matching, which proves the content sets are equal. Or the original zip is published as a release asset, which is the only way to satisfy the digest itself.
