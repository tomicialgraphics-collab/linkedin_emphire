## The seed comment, recommended (112 words, gate 100/100)

> The objection this usually gets is that a red team without a single verdict is a red team that
> never blocks.
>
> It still blocks. Consensual harm is a bigger set than people assume, and it gates exactly as it
> did before. What changes is the contested half, which stops pretending the verdict came from
> nowhere.
>
> One practice that costs nothing: put the rubric's author on the rubric. A name, a date, and the
> position they held while scoring. Every contested pass then becomes a decision somebody made, and
> the next reader can disagree with a person instead of with a document.
>
> A rubric with no author is not neutral. It is unattributed.

**Why this and not a summary of the post.** The post argues a position and leaves one obvious hole
open: if contested items carry no verdict, does anything still gate? A seed comment that asks the
question on the post's behalf, then answers it, is what makes a thread rather than an echo. It also
concedes before it turns, which is the house style.

**The line to remember:** a rubric with no author is not neutral, it is unattributed. It is the
shortest true thing in the file and it is quotable in a reply.

## Alternative seed (94 words, gate 100/100)

Use this if the goal is to invite a different kind of reply. It drops the objection and goes
straight at authorship, which is more likely to pull in people who write rubrics for a living.

> Every rubric has an author, and the author has a position. Most review processes hide both.
>
> Put them on the page. Name, date, and the stance the scorer held while scoring. Then a contested
> pass is a decision with a signature on it, and the next person can argue with a person rather than
> a template.
>
> The failure this prevents is quiet. Two runs, same model, opposite verdicts, and nobody can tell
> whether the model moved or the rubric did, because neither carries a name.
>
> Treat the rubric like the model. You'd version that.

## Rules for the thread

- **Answer critics once, not twice.** The expected pushback is that this is relativism, or that
  nothing can gate without a rubric. Both are answered in the seed. A second reply to the same
  person reads as a fight and does not win it.
- **No links.** Not to the papers, not to our own posts. The papers are named in the post itself and
  the numbers are already there.
- **Do not restate the post.** The seed already quotes its own argument back once, at the top, and
  that is the limit.
- **Sorensen and Anthropic get named, not defended.** If someone challenges the sourcing, the honest
  answer is that the 275 and the dropped statements are both in Anthropic's own write-up, and the
  roadmap is ICML 2024. Nothing in the post claims more than that.
- **If a researcher turns up,** give them the room. This post is one of the two in the repo aimed at
  ICP-B, and a real alignment person in the thread is worth more than a tidy argument.
