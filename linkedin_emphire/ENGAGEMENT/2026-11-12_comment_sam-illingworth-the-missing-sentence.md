## Comment, paste as is

Publishing that a tool exists while publishing nothing about what it gets wrong is the pattern, and the register is the evidence. There's a field for the tool and a field for the caseworker's duty. None of them would hold a measured error rate.

On child safeguarding the risk isn't a wrong sentence. It's a missing one. A cited summary lets a caseworker check what it said. Nothing in that design lets them notice what it left out, and the duty to validate against source is hardest to perform at the hour of highest caseload.

There is a cheap test for it. Take referrals that already closed, hand a second caseworker the summary alone, and ask which fact decided the case. Then count how often that fact is not in the summary. No lab required, it runs on records you already hold, and it's the number that says whether the tool is helping or quietly setting the frame.

What belongs in that record is not an accuracy score. Rather, it's how often the summary changed a decision, and how often it dropped the fact that mattered.
