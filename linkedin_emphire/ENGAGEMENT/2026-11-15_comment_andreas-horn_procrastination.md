# Comment on Andreas Horn's procrastination post, 15 November 2026

What this is: one comment for his post, adding the version of the trap he did not name. What to do next: paste it
once. No link, no pitch, and no connection request because he is at his limit.

## Why this angle

The post is about the conversation founders avoid with buyers. The near-identical conversation is the one with
somebody trying to break the product, and it is avoided for the same reason: another feature is more comfortable
and looks like progress.

That version is not a metaphor here. It is what the owner did to his own chatbot, and the result is the kind of
evidence his audience respects: six attempts, four failures, found in an afternoon rather than a launch.

The ending is deliberately flat. "I would have called the release a success and gone back to building" admits the
thing the post is accusing people of, which is worth more under a post about self-deception than any line about
how others should behave.

## The count, before the block

**776 characters, 136 words.** Well inside the 1,250 limit, short enough to read in the feed.

## Comment, paste this

The trap has a second door. Building is also how founders avoid the afternoon where somebody tries to break the
thing.

A demo looks finished because nobody attacked it. The landing page, the pricing, the onboarding flow all assume
the visitor does what the flow says. An hour spent trying to make it misbehave is the same kind of work as the
customer call: uncomfortable, cheap, and impossible to fake with another feature.

I ran six injection attempts against my own chatbot and four worked, including a leaked system prompt and a
fabricated refund triggered by a single word. None of that shows up in the happy path, and finding it cost an
afternoon rather than a launch.

Those four would have shipped. I would have called the release a success and gone back to building.
