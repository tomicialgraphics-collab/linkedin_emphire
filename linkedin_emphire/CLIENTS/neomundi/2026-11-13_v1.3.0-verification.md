# NeoMundi conformance corpus 1.3.0: what was checked here, and what was not

Sébastien Favre reported on 13 November at 23:09 and 23:12 that PR #39 is merged, the corpus is at v1.3.0, both
observations were reproduced, and the fail-closed default remains a technical disagreement. This is the verification of
those claims, run before answering him.

## Checked, and true

| Claim | How it was checked | Result |
|---|---|---|
| PR #39 merged | `gh pr view 39` | merged 2026-11-13 22:07 UTC, 69 files, +1,334 and -348 |
| Corpus at v1.3.0 | `conformance/VERSION` in a fresh clone | 1.3.0 |
| Fifty cases, unchanged fixtures | `runners/build_fixtures.py --check` | generator reproduces the corpus byte for byte, 121 files, 50 sheets |
| Digests conform | `runners/run.py`, digest phase | 172 files verified before the case run |
| Eleven CI checks pass | `gh pr checks 39` | 11 pass, including a new job whose name is "sans cryptography" followed by "NOT TESTED, jamais PASS" |
| Dependency diagnostics fixed | `runners/run.py` in an environment with no Rust binaries | stops and names the two missing binaries and the two build commands |

The last two are the fix to his observation about a missing dependency reading as a regression. The CI job names the
property rather than the symptom, which is the stronger form.

## Not checked here

The case-by-case run needs the two Rust verifiers, `evidence-verify-rs` and `evidence-verify-crypto`, which this sandbox
does not carry. So the fifty verdicts were not re-measured on this machine, and nothing here claims they were. What is
verified is the corpus, the digests, the generator, the prerequisite handling and the CI result.

The two patch files sent to him on 13 November were written against 1.2.0. They will not apply at 1.3.0, and they are
not being resent.

## What the release itself records

The changelog says the flag propagation was not in my report. Investigating my two observations is what surfaced it:

> The corpus declared a strict-mode value that no run had ever measured. `run.py` appended `--strict` to five of its
> seven command paths. On those, `run.py --strict` compared the sheet's `exit_code_strict` against a tool launched in
> non-strict mode, so the three wrong values could not surface, and CI stayed green on a claim it was not testing.

Three sheets moved their `exit_code_strict` from 0 to 2: A-05, F-03 and A-07. No fixture byte changed, no verdict
changed, and no default exit code changed. That last one is the disagreement, untouched and explicit.

## What the write-up has to carry

1. Both observations, each with the reproduction and the outcome: one intentional and under-documented, one real at the
   bench level.
2. The flag-propagation defect, credited to the corpus rather than to the review, because it was found by the maintainer
   while checking the review.
3. The fail-closed proposal as a design alternative, with the maintainer's contract reasoning stated at full strength,
   and the reviewer's remaining position stated as a preference about defaults and about what an exit code means to a
   caller that reads nothing else.
4. The assurance boundary in the corpus's own words: integrity is not truth, a trust store declares identities, and a
   corpus finds counter-examples rather than proving their absence.
