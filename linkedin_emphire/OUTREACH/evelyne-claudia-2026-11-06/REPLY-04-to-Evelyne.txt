The terminology is in. Where the two records differ in value, sequence 4 BLOCK against sequence 5 ALLOW, that is
the wire pair and the disagreement is in the verdict value. Where both records carry BLOCK and the difference
is in the descriptor and the decision time, that is the host pair and the disagreement is between two different
signed verdict records. Correction 02 said the host cells were two different verdicts for one action id, which
was accurate but flat, and the new note supersedes it wherever the two comparisons sit side by side.

You were right that a matching patch line is not a file check, and I had been treating it as one. The runner now
verifies the SHA-256 of store, host and common against the hashes this case names, applies the patch, verifies
the patched store against the hash the first run printed, and refuses to measure anything if either check fails.
A guard earns its place through a negative control, because one that has never refused proves nothing. A single
comment line was appended to a copy of store.py, a change nobody would notice in review, and the runner stopped
with the expected and actual hashes in the message and exit code 1. The first runner stays as it is, since its
hash is already in your hands and editing it would break the artifact you hold rather than improve it.

The verdict-write refusal is measured now, in one run, on one copy of the benchmark, beside the conflict refusal.
Your five surfaces are in the order you gave them and the table is in the note. The short form: the refused reply
is byte-identical in both cells, the count of durable records is two against zero, and the state a replay recomputes
is conflicted in one and null in the other.

There is one result I did not expect and it belongs in the message rather than a footnote. My written expectation
predicted the host diagnostics would differ between the two refusals. They do not. The host records wal_commit,
reply_rejected and effect_withheld in both, so the host's own account of what happened fails to separate them in
exactly the way the reply does. The surfaces that do separate the two are the ones a reader has to go looking for:
the durable records, the count of accepted writes, and the replayed state. Everything handed to a consumer at the
moment of the refusal looks the same, and that is a stronger statement than the one I wrote down.

The claim I will keep is the smaller one. A refused conflict write is reported in the words of a refused verdict
write. I will not enlarge that into the two being indistinguishable, because they are distinguishable, just not by
anything the system says while it is happening.

The phrase you asked me to drop is gone. Validation is earned by the evidence, bounded by the tested claim, and
open to correction, including your own reading of your own work. It was never mine to hand over.
