# Comment on Marcus Chan's post, 8 November 2026

## The judgement first

This one is reach, not fit. He sells sales systems to founders, my work is agent evidence for engineers, and the overlap
is one idea rather than a market. A comment here is worth posting because his audience is large and his post is a genuine
argument, not because it will produce a client.

His closing call is to comment SCALE. Not doing that, and not mentioning it. A person who buys sales systems can see a
keyword reply from a mile away.

## Why the angle works anyway

His argument is about friction, and friction is the wrong name for the thing he is defending. What he is actually
protecting is authorship: a number a person typed is a claim they can be asked about, and a number a system produced is a
claim nobody can defend. That is his point said more precisely, and it is the same distinction that decides whether an
agent's account of its own work is evidence or decoration.

## Comment

Agreed, and the part worth pushing on is that the typing isn't the thing. Authorship is. A number a person entered by hand
is a claim they can be asked about. A dashboard is a claim nobody can defend.

The same rule decides what an agent leaves behind. The useful artefact isn't the dashboard of calls assembled afterwards,
it's what the agent recorded at the moment it acted, before the outcome was known. An after-the-fact summary is a trend. A
claim made at the time is something you can coach.

## What not to say

No SCALE. No disagreement about the value of friction, because he has argued it well and the comment is stronger agreeing
and sharpening. No mention of agents, red teaming or governance in the first sentence, since a technical opener under a
sales post reads as an advert for a different conversation.

## Held for later

Nothing. This is a one-comment relationship at most, and if it produces anything it will be a rep or an owner arriving at
the profile, which is the only reason to be in that thread at all.
