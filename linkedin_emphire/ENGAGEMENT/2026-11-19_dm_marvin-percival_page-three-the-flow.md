Page three, the flow. Your two corrections first, because they change what the record even is.

"Who it was shown to" is gone, and you were right that it was doing two jobs at once and badly. What the record holds now is a link and a date: shown through link 4821, opened on 3 March. No name. I can say when it was opened without ever knowing it was Ada, and if a lawyer asks who saw it, the honest answer is that we know a link was opened from her number, not who held the phone.

On deletion, your sentence is better than mine and I am keeping it close to yours: if you delete your file, we remove the paper, and we keep a note that a paper with this fingerprint was registered on this date by this agency. That goes in front of people at sign-up, not in a policy page nobody opens. Whether that note survives an erasure request is a question for a lawyer before a real user, and I am not going to answer it myself.

Now Ada, a plot in Lekki, and the survey plan sent to her on WhatsApp.

Minute one. She opens the check page on her phone. No account, no app. She drags the PDF in or taps the link the agent already sent her.

Minute two. Her phone works out the fingerprint. The file is never uploaded to me for a check, which is the smallest amount of other people's property I can hold and still answer the question. The phone asks the register one thing: do you hold this fingerprint.

Minute three. Four possible answers, and no fifth:
No record under this fingerprint.
Registered, level 1: registered on 3 March by Lekki Homes Agency, not confirmed by anyone else.
Registered, level 2: matches the copy Adebayo Surveys registered on 2 March.
Disputed, level 3: two versions exist. Here is the first, here is the second.

Minute four. The same line sits under all four answers, above the buttons: this does not mean the plot is safe to buy, and it does not mean the paper is genuine. She reads that before she reads the result.

Minute five. She forwards the record link to the person putting money in, and that is the whole of the buyer's side. The record is the thing she keeps.

A year later her lawyer opens that link. Fingerprint, the date, who registered it, and that the surveyor's own copy matched. He also sees that no second version has been registered since, and if one had, he would see both in order with neither marked true.

One weakness I should say out loud. None of this exists until an agency or a surveyor registers, so the buyer's check has to be free and the agency pays. That is not generosity. The agency is the one who needs to be believed, and a buyer will not pay to be told there is no record.
