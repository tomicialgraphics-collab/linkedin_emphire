Measurable is the word I'd push on, because it's the one everybody says and almost nobody defines.

Completion rates measure attendance. Self-reported confidence measures enthusiasm. The thing worth measuring is whether
somebody can tell when the model is confidently wrong, and show the check they ran to find out, because that is exactly
what the work costs when it goes wrong.

The cheapest instrument for that isn't a quiz. It's a task, a deadline, and a reviewer who doesn't know whose work
they're looking at. Blind, and with a rule that the person has to show their working.

That connects to your first line. If teams are people and agents, measurement turns into accountability: when the agent
produces the deliverable, who signs it, and what can they show about how it was checked.

The Build Sessions are the ones I'd watch for that reason. Building is where the gap between knowing and being able to
show it gets found.
