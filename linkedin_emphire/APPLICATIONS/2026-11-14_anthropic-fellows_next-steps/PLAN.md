## The six things that decide the score

**1. The five hours do not start until the begin form.** Everything before it is free: the two papers, the environment, the deck's skeleton, the experiment idea, the video plan. Reading *Studying AI Welfare Empirically* and the global workspace paper is a six to eight hour job and none of it is on the clock. Doing that first is the difference between five hours of design and five hours of panic.

**2. Missing transcripts are disqualification.** AI assistance is expected and must be submitted in full, chat and agent logs, redacted for keys. That includes any help taken from an assistant, so the transcript capture has to be switched on at the start and kept on, rather than reconstructed afterwards.

**3. The numbered slides must be the owner's own words.** Appendix slides and figures may be AI-drafted, and automated detection is run on the numbered ones. The division of labour is therefore explicit: the argument in the owner's prose, the apparatus drafted.

**4. CodeSignal is the opposite regime.** No AI, no web search, no external editor, no pasting, proctored with webcam and photo ID, one attempt, no retakes for technical failure on the candidate's side. The unit tests are the final word on an ambiguous spec, and only passing tests are graded, so style is irrelevant and time is everything. **Nothing from that page gets pasted into any assistant while it is open.** A takehome transcript that contains CodeSignal content reads as AI use on CodeSignal.

**5. The references are the only part that depends on other people.** They were emailed on 13 November, they are due on the same deadline, and the emails can land in spam. Praveen and Oladimeji need one message each today, and a second only if they go quiet by the 20th.

**6. The deadline is not the only date in this.** A January 18 start in California is a visa question that is priced in weeks, not days. That decision is worth taking before the assessments are finished, not after a decision arrives in December.
