## Comment, paste as is

<!-- gate:body:start -->
The list is a better instrument than most people's AI strategy, and it picks up one category the post doesn't name: items that are still there because nobody has ever written down what done looks like. No eval fixes those, and they're the cheapest to fix.

On the email review, I'd skip the new eval to start with. Write down what you actually checked in the last twenty emails you sent, in the order you checked it. That list is the eval, you've already paid for it, and it usually collapses into three items rather than the feeling of doesn't quite sound like me.

Then the question that decides whether to keep reviewing at all: in the past thirty days, has that review ever rejected something? A review that hasn't said no is a keystroke. It's harder to spot than an unreviewed queue, because somebody did look.

The Bobby item is where the list earns its keep. Waiting on a person isn't an AI gap, it's a handoff gap, and it's the one item on your list that more automation won't shrink.

## Why the missing category is worth saying

Her method already distinguishes three reasons an item survives the day: not good enough, not trusted, never going to be good enough. The fourth is the most common one in practice and the only one that isn't about the model, which makes it the one an AI-first course cannot teach and a reader can fix this week.

## Why the email exercise beats another eval

Building an eval is a project. Writing down what you checked on your previous twenty sends is an hour, and it produces the same artefact: a list of failure conditions in the order they matter to you. Recommending the cheaper route to the same object is more useful to her audience than recommending the impressive one.

## Why the reject question is the sharpest line

It converts a feeling about a workflow into a measurement, and it is the only test here that produces a yes or a no on the spot. A review that has never rejected anything is doing nothing while looking like diligence, and the reason it survives is that it has a witness.

## Why the close is about the handoff

Her own example is the one place where adding AI makes the queue longer rather than shorter, and saying so is more valuable than a fourth endorsement of automation. It also leaves her with the strongest of her own points rather than a replacement for it.
