# Repost of Sam Illingworth's AI feedback post, 14 November 2026

## Why a repost and not a comment

The comment drafted earlier answers Sam. A repost answers my own feed, which is founders and engineering leads shipping an LLM feature with paying customers. They will not read the paper. They will read four lines and decide whether their own dashboard has the same defect, so the thought has to be about their product rather than about the study.

## What the repost adds

The post says usage can be bought with synthetic warmth. That is true and it is a warning. The pricing makes it usable: the study's own mediators show competence carrying 38 percent of the effect on achievement, autonomy 34 percent on engagement, relatedness 22 percent on persistence. The encouraging arm moved relatedness alone, which is why it produced returns and no learning. A reader can now ask which of the three their product moves, and whether anything they have measures it.

Nothing here contradicts the post, nothing needs Sam to reply, and the repost itself carries his name and his text above my lines.

## Repost to paste

An AI tutor that only encourages kept students coming back for eight weeks, and did not make them better at the work.

The study behind this post priced that difference. Competence carries 38% of the feedback effect on achievement, autonomy 34% on engagement, relatedness 22% on persistence. Encouraging feedback strengthened relatedness alone, so it was loading the 22% path while the 38% path sat still.

Most AI product pitches I read lead with the number that arm maximised. That is not a coincidence. One of those numbers is cheap to count and the other needs a before and after.

If your feature has users and you cannot say which of those three needs it moves, the retention number is standing in for a claim nobody made.
