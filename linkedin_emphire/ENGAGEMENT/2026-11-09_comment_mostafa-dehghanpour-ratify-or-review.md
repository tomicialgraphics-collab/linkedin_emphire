## The angle

He is asking a question the industry answers with introspection, and introspection is the one instrument that cannot see
automation bias. People sincerely report that they scrutinised. The record shows what they accepted.

So give him the observable instead of the worry: the rate at which the human changes or rejects what the chain handed them. It
is the only measure that distinguishes reviewing from ratifying, it is cheap to compute, and a quarterly zero is a finding.

Then the second half, which is why his chain of two systems makes it worse. A second opinion requires a second source of
evidence. If the check reads the same document as the draft, the agreement is one opinion counted twice, and it arrives at
the human wearing the costume of confirmation.

## Comment, paste as is

One figure tells you whether the person at the end is reviewing or ratifying, and it's almost never in the evidence pack: the rate at which they change the chain's answer. A quarter of zeroes means nobody is reviewing. The signature is documenting a decision made three steps earlier, while the regulation treats that signature as the control.

The chain makes it harder still. A second system agreeing counts as a second opinion only if it saw different evidence. If the check reads the same document as the draft, its agreement is one opinion counted twice, and it reaches the human looking like confirmation.
