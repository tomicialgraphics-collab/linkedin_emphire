# Scorecard, 18 November

Read from the screenshot at 23:19 WAT on 18 November. Past seven days.

| Number | 14 Nov | 18 Nov | Change |

| Search appearances | 11 | 31 | +20, 2.8x |

## The test I set, and whether it passed

The audit of 14 November ended with one instruction: look at two numbers a week later, and search appearances should move off 11 while views hold above 500.

Search moved. 11 to 31 in four days is not a rounding error, and the only keyword surface changed in that window was the headline, from the five-item version to the 138-character one. Views held, and the view rate improved, which means the people who arrived found something worth stopping for.

So the headline change was right and the discoverability problem is smaller than it was.

## The number that still says no

31 searches in a week is about four a day. Nobody buys an AI red teaming engagement by searching for it in Lagos twice a day. Search was worth fixing because it is free, not because it is the channel.

The channel is the feed. 10,783 impressions against 31 searches is a ratio of roughly 350 to one, and it means the work is done by what he publishes, not by what he is indexed for. Four straight days of that traffic and 645 views is the same conversion rate as before the change.

Read together, those two lines say: stop optimising the profile for search. It is as good as it is going to get without changing the offer, and the offer is not the thing to change four days in.

## The one surface that has not moved since 14 November

The banner. It still carries two photographs of the same person, a bubble that reads "Let's break it first" at a size nobody can read in a feed, and a claim that repeats the headline in different words.

Every other surface from that audit was changed: services, headline, About, featured order, skills. The banner is the one that was written up and left alone, and it is the largest object on the page.

One photograph, the claim, three domains. 1584 by 396. It takes an hour and it is the only item left on the list.

## The typo to check before anything else

The company line under the name reads "Teartm.inc". The experience section, as recorded on 14 November, says Teamart from March 2024. If that string is meant to be the same company, the top of the most-read page in his business carries a misspelling of it, and that is a five-second fix he can make before he reads anything else here.

## What I would not do this week

Do not change the headline again. A keyword change needs a fortnight to read, and changing it now would throw away the one clean movement in the table.

Do not chase impressions. 10,783 against 11,055 is noise, and a post that reads the analytics publicly would be telling people about the scoreboard instead of the work.

Do not rebuild the Featured section. It was reordered on the audit's advice and there is no evidence yet either way.
