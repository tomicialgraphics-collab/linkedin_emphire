# Comment on Ricky Jones's Kettle Culture post, 15 November 2026

What this is: an answer to the question he poses in the last third of the post, after the comedy. What to do next:
paste once. No link, no pitch.

## Why this angle

The post contains the question: who authorised which action, on what object, and is that authority still valid when
the action happens. He also supplies two of the failure modes, a rename that does not revoke and an old approval
that does not expire. Nobody in the thread has yet said what the mechanism is, and the mechanism is the whole
answer.

That mechanism is scope. Authority belongs to the request, not to the actor or to the moment. An action, an
object, a limit, a window. Once authority is written that way, the rename and the stale approval stop being edge
cases and become the same failure: a grant that was recorded once and is being used later, against something it was
never evaluated against.

The 14 November comment on this thread was about the committee. This one does not return to it. It ends where a brake
actually sits, which is the door, and hands the clipboard back to Gerald as a souvenir.

## Comment, paste this

Since Gerald's appeal failed for want of authority, here's the part I'd put in the code.

Authority shouldn't be a property of a person or of a moment. It should be a property of the request: this action,
on this object, within this limit, before this time. A grant naming Steven is not a grant naming whatever he calls
himself next week, unless it was written to follow the seat rather than the man. That distinction is a design
choice, and most systems make it silently.

Which is why an old approval stops being permission the moment nothing can evaluate it. If the only record of
the authorisation is a minute, the question at action time is not whether it was approved. It is whether anything
can still check that it applies, and a rename, a reorg or a new version of the object all answer no.

So the brake is smaller than it looks in the song. Not a committee, not a person with a clipboard. It is the check
at the door that refuses when the grant doesn't match the request standing in front of it. Gerald can keep the
larger clipboard. It just shouldn't be the thing that opens the door.
