# Comment on Love Babbar's post, 8 November 2026

## Why this one

A personal story from someone with an enormous education audience, and the story is genuinely well told. The comment should
not compete with it or extract a lesson for the commenter's own field. It should show that the post was read properly,
which means noticing which decision he actually made.

Everyone will read this as leaving a high salary. He says plainly that the problem was that someone else held the switch on
his own work, which is a control question, not a risk appetite question. That distinction is worth naming because it is the
thing that transfers to anyone reading who has a smaller version of the same situation.

## Comment

The salary is the part people will read, and it is not the decision. You left because someone else held the switch on your
own work, and that is a control question rather than a risk question.

Which is why your test holds up better than the usual advice. Most decision advice asks whether you can explain the choice,
which is a question about the listener. Yours asks what the floor looks like, which is a question about you, and it is
answerable on a bad day.

That sentence is worth teaching.

## What not to say

No praise opening, no "so inspiring", no mention of the money as though it were the brave part. No theological or
philosophical extension, and nothing about my own work. No question at the end, since the post does not ask one and
appending one would turn a story into a prompt.

## Held for nothing

There is no reply worth engineering. If he answers, a thanks is enough, and the relationship value here is reach rather
than conversation.
