What this is
Thirteen screens that show what the practice build should look like when it works. They are pictures, not
code. Their job is to give Timothy and Marvin one shared view of every screen before the first line of code,
so arguments happen over a picture and not over a memory.
The screens follow one story. A verifier signs in with a passkey, checks a NIPOST postcode, records an
elderly speaker in Yoruba with her consent, adds a GPS-stamped photo and a document, gets each item
attested by someone other than the speaker, and seals the file. The fingerprints are anchored through
FORAY. A one-page certificate with a QR code lets anyone check the file on their own phone, without an
account.

Part 1 · The verifier's flow
1 Sign in with a passkey
The first screen a verifier sees. One main button: sign in with a
passkey (Stele). There is no email, username or password field
anywhere. A second button creates a passkey for a new verifier. A
link lets a member of the public check a certificate without signing
in.
The English / Yorùbá switch is at the top of every screen.
Check in the test
Sign-in works with the phone's screen lock on both the Android phone
and the iPhone. No field anywhere asks for an email.
2 Postcode and NIPOST lookup
The verifier types the postcode from the building's NIPOST plate.
The server, not the phone, asks NIPOST Lookup and shows the
official address, building use and time of the check. Ẹ̀rí stores the
postcode, NIPOST's answer and the time.
If NIPOST does not find the postcode, the file cannot start. Ẹ̀rí never
guesses a postcode from a map or an address.
Check in the test
The address appears within 3 seconds on 4G. The NIPOST key appears
nowhere in the page or the response. "Not found" and "NIPOST
unreachable" each show their own message.

3 The evidence file
One file per postcode. Each item shows its type, who it is from, who
attested it and its fingerprint. The document item is marked in
amber because it has no attestation yet, and it blocks sealing until it
has one.
The note under the list carries the core rule: once fingerprinted,
nothing is edited or deleted. A correction or a disagreement is
added as something new.
Check in the test
A direct attempt to edit a stored item is refused. The seal button
refuses while any item lacks an attestation.
4 Voice testimony, consent first
The recording has two parts. First the speaker says, in her own
words and her own language, who she is and that she agrees the
recording is kept and can be heard by people who check the
certificate. Then she gives her testimony. The screen marks where
the consent sits in the recording.
The verifier ticks a box confirming they heard the consent. Under
Timothy's REQ-046, the spoken consent is the record and the tick
box only supports it.
Check in the test
A ten-minute recording on both phones. The consent is audible in the
first seconds. The fingerprint is computed on the phone and matches
the server's.

5 Photo with GPS
The camera stamps the phone's position, its accuracy and the time
at the moment of capture. None of these can be typed in. The
verifier picks what the photo shows: the house, the postcode plate
or a document.
The position is where the phone was, accurate to the figure shown.
It is not a boundary. Timothy's REQ-014 adds a caption saying
exactly that, because in a Lagos layout eight metres can be the
neighbour's plot.
Check in the test
Location permission works on both phones. A photo taken with
location turned off is refused or clearly marked.
6 Attestation
A named person, other than the speaker, confirms an item. The
verifier records how they relate to the household (family, neighbour
or independent) and what they can confirm:
Presence: was there when it was recorded
Knowledge: knows the facts first-hand
Independent: has no tie to the household
Verifier: the signed-in verifier
The statement is recorded by voice or typed. If the person
disagrees, the link at the bottom adds a counter-attestation
instead.
Check in the test
The speaker's own name is refused as attestor. A counter-attestation
appears beside the original, which stays unchanged.

7 Review and seal
Before sealing, four checks must pass: postcode checked, consent
heard on every voice item, every item attested by someone other
than the speaker, and fingerprints matching on phone and server.
The screen lists the fingerprints that go to FORAY. Only fingerprints
leave the server. Recordings, photos and names do not. The amber
warning says plainly that the batch cannot change after sealing.
Check in the test
One seal sends one batch. The transaction ID and block time are
stored and open on a public explorer. A forced FORAY failure leaves
nothing marked sealed (Timothy's REQ-049).
Not yet drawn in Part 1
Timothy's amendments add states the v0.1 screens do not show yet. They go into v0.2 if he and Marvin
accept them:
REQ-047: a separate, per-person consent to be named, dated and revocable, on the voice screen
REQ-048: an attestor withdrawing, shown as a new event beside the item
REQ-049: the seal screen when FORAY fails, with a retry
REQ-050: the verifier's organisation statement, recorded at sign-up and printed on the certificate
REQ-014: the position caption under every photo

Part 2 · What others see
8 The certificate
One A4 page. It carries the building, postcode, verifier, sealing time and anchor reference, a table of
every item with who gave it, who attested it and its fingerprint, and three steps for checking. The QR
code opens the check page. The disclaimer sits in a box at the foot of the page and cannot be changed
by any brand.
Private people appear by their role, such as "Speaker (family)". Their names appear only where they
agreed to be named. Professionals, such as the verifier, are always named (REQ-038).

9 Public check: match
What anyone sees after scanning the QR code: no sign-in, no app. A
green panel says all items match what was sealed, and when. Below
it are the building, the verifier, the anchor reference (which opens
on a public explorer) and each item marked "Matches".
"Test my own copy of a file" lets a checker pick a file on their phone.
It is compared on the phone and never uploaded.
Check in the test
This is the minute Timothy's plan is built around: a stranger with a file,
no account and no app, is told whether it matches and can open the
anchor herself.
10 Public check: no match
The checker's file does not match any sealed item. The screen says
so plainly, shows both fingerprints, and explains the usual reason:
even a resized or re-sent photo gives a different fingerprint. It tells
the checker to ask for the original.
Check in the test
A copy altered by one byte says "no match". The deliberately broken
seed item (Timothy's REQ-051) fails on every release.

11 The check page in Yoruba
The same page as screen 9 with the switch set to YO. All words on
screen come from a translation file. Only the language changes,
never the layout or the evidence.
Draft text. These Yoruba strings were written for the mockup and
have not been checked by a speaker. Timothy corrects them, including
tone marks, before the screen is shown to anyone.
12 Brand configuration
The product name, tagline, logo, main colour, interface languages
and certificate footer all come from one brand file. Another country
or a licensee can run the same software under its own name.
The disclaimer is the one thing a brand cannot change.
Check in the test
Changing the brand file changes the name and colour on every screen
and the certificate with no code change.
