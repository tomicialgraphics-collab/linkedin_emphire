# What the 583 profile viewers say about who is actually arriving

Source: the viewer page he pasted on 13 November, transcribed to
`analytics/profile-viewers/2026-11-13_visible.txt` and counted with `./emphire viewers`. LinkedIn named three viewers
and described the rest in the anonymous form, which is still a description of role, industry and place.

**Read this as a share, not a census.** 189 descriptors were visible out of 583 views in ninety days. The proportions
below are the visible third, and they are enough to describe the shape.

## The counts

| Segment | Count | Share of visible |
|---|---|---|
| Founder or business owner | 45 | 24% |
| University, college or student | 32 | 17% |
| Named engineering role at a company | 17 | 9% |
| Security industry or security vendor | 15 | 8% |
| Research fellow or professor | 7 | 4% |

Industries named most often: Software Development 30, Technology, Information and Internet 8, IT Services and IT
Consulting 7, Computer and Network Security 5, Research Services 4.

Founders inside a software, IT or security industry: **29 of 45**.

Scaled to the whole 583, and this is arithmetic on a sample rather than a measurement: about 139 founders, about 89 of
them in software, IT or security, about 99 students or university staff, about 47 people inside security vendors.

## Five readings

**1. One in four is a founder, which validates the targeting and explains nothing.** The dream buyer in the strategy is a
founder or engineering lead shipping an LLM feature. The audience is founders. But the descriptor stops at "Founder in
the Software Development industry", and that includes the one person with a fine-tuned wrapper and no budget and the
agency owner shopping for someone to subcontract. It does not tell you which one is which. Only a conversation does.

**2. About one in six is a student or university account, and that is your comment footprint talking.** Thirty two of 189
is not a rounding error; it is what a comment thread on a campus, career, or "AI is changing everything" post produces.
Those views are not neutral. They depress the ratio that decides whether a real buyer sees a peer or a crowd.

**3. Eight percent work inside security vendors.** Cymulate, Gray Swan, Sonatype, Palo Alto Networks, Advai, Tenet
Security, Nimble, InfraGard. Every one of those is a competitor, a tool vendor, or a professional association. They read
you and they will never hire you to test their own product. That is not a failure, it is the category: AI security
content recruits AI security people. The buyers are the founders in row one, and they are the ones who do not comment.

**4. The geography is flat, so stop looking for a local market.** United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, Germany,
Spain, Sweden, Poland, Romania, Greece, India, South Korea, Taiwan, Malaysia, Singapore, Australia, Israel, Saudi Arabia,
Brazil, Mexico, Nigeria once. Which means the offer has to be remote, quoted in dollars, and hand off to a call in a
timezone that works for California and for Singapore. It already is.

**5. You can name three of them.** Free LinkedIn names three viewers and describes the rest. Premium unlocks the list. A
one month trial at zero naira is the cheapest qualified list available to you anywhere, and it is the only one built from
people who already looked.

## What to do about it

**Take the Premium month, use it as a data pull, cancel it.** The value is not the badge. It is the ability to export
about 139 founders into a list, most of whom are already interested enough to click a profile. Add the cancel date to the
open loops on the day you start, because the trial reminds you seven days out and the reminder is the only thing between
you and a charge.

**Triage, do not broadcast.** Keep founders, CTOs and engineering leads at software or security companies with a shipped
AI feature. Drop students, academics, vendor employees and recruiters. Roughly 30 percent survives, so about 40 people
out of 139, which is a realistic month of hand written messages.

**Message only where you can name their system.** Three lines, no link, no attachment in the first message:

```
You shipped the [product] assistant recently. The thing I would test first is what happens when someone tells it to ignore its own rules, because that is the one that gets found by a customer rather than a reviewer.

I keep a one page checklist of the classes worth trying before a launch. Want it?
```

The rule that keeps this from being spam: if you cannot name what their product does, you do not have a reason to write,
and the message waits until you do.

**Stop commenting where the students are.** The thirty two university views came from somewhere. Campus career threads,
"how do I get into AI" posts, and generic industry cheerleading all convert to views from people who will never buy.
Founder posts, launch posts, and post mortems convert to views from people who might.

**Log it monthly and compare.** `./emphire viewers` reruns the same counts on the next pasted list. The number to watch is
not 583. It is the founder share of the visible list going up while the student share goes down, because that is the only
movement that means the arrival channel changed rather than the volume.
