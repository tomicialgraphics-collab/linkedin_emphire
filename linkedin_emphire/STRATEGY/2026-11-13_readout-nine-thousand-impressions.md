# The week of 7 to 13 November: 9,192 impressions, and what it actually changed

Own analytics, exported 13 November 2026. Everything below is measured, not inferred, except where it says so.

## What the numbers are

| Measure | This week | Prior week | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Post impressions | 9,192 | about 1,945 | +373% |
| Followers | 1,148 | 1,007 on 6 November | +141 over the week |
| Profile views (90 day) | 572 | 400 | +43% |
| Members reached | 5,651 | not given | |
| Posts published | 9 | 1 to 2 | |
| Comments sent | 105 | 6 drafted, 0 confirmed | |
| Social engagements | 23 | | 12 reactions, 9 comments, 0 reposts, 2 saves |
| Out-of-network share | 87% | | |

Per post, which is where the useful part is:

- **Nvidia, 12 November: 7,241 impressions, 98% out-of-network, 4,967 members reached, 1 save.**
- Case study, 13 November: 47 impressions at the time of reading, 27 reached.
- Freshness clock, 12 November: 185.
- The case study "score survived the argument" post: 740.
- Exact match: 307.

## The three findings

**One. News travels; explanation does not, at the same size.** A post that reports an event and then makes one
point did 7,241. The three technical posts, all of which are arguably better written, did 307, 740 and 185. That
is not a quality gap, it is a distribution gap: the news post was handed to people who do not follow the
account, at 98 percent out-of-network. The technical posts reached the network and stopped.

**Two. Two posts in twelve hours killed the second one.** The case study post went out the same night as a post
still being distributed. It got 47 impressions. The format is not the problem: the same post, on a quiet day,
is a normal 500 to 1,500 impression post. The account competed with itself, and the newer post lost. One post
per 24 hours is not a preference, it is the operating rule the data just wrote.

**Three. Engagement rate is now the weak number, not reach.** 23 engagements across 9,192 impressions is 0.25
percent, and 0 reposts. Reach arrived and nothing was passed on. The likely reason is that every post ends on a
claim, and a claim is quotable but not usable. What engineers repost is a thing they can run.

## The audience and the buyer are not the same people

Reach demographics: 46 percent at companies of 10,001 or more, 30 percent software development, 22 percent
software engineers, 17 percent San Francisco Bay Area, 3 percent Google, 3 percent Nvidia.

Follower demographics: 13 percent founders, 18 percent at firms of 2 to 10 people, 20 percent IT services and
consulting, 35 percent senior.

So the posts are reaching employees at large technology companies, and the followers are founders and
consultants at small firms. The first group reads for interest and does not buy independent review. The second
group does. That argues for keeping the news format for reach and writing the posts that convert for the
follower base, which is a different voice and a shorter post.

## What changes on 14 November

1. **One post a day, minimum 24 hours apart.** No exceptions, including when the case study or a client message
   is waiting.
2. **Two news-shaped posts a week, three at most.** They carry reach. They do not carry revenue.
3. **Every post ships something runnable in the first comment**, and the post says so in one clause. A checklist,
   the playbook, the PDF. Reposts come from utility, not agreement.
4. **Three comments a day on posts with real engagement**, using the correction shape that has already produced
   the best numbers: take a figure in someone else's post, say what it does and does not measure, give the
   stronger version.
5. **Fill `analytics/follower-log.md` at 20:00 daily.** The 15 November checkpoint needs 158 a day to reach 1,600
   and will be missed. The number to carry instead is 1,994 projected on the deadline, and the only thing that
   moves it is comment volume on other people's posts.

## What is not measurable yet, and would be worth having

No attribution for followers by source. No count of DMs opened from profile views, which is the step that turns
572 profile views into conversations. And no per-post record of how many profile views each post produced, which
is the number this lane should be optimising: impressions are a vanity figure, profile views are the doorway,
and DMs are the money.
