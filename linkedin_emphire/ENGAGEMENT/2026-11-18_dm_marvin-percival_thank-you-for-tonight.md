## Message, paste as is

<!-- gate:body:start -->
Marvin, thank you for calling rather than typing. It isn't often I get to talk to somebody who started writing code at nineteen and is still building something that didn't exist when he started.

What you said about the work landed differently because of the two corrections you've already put into my register. Praise from somebody who has read it that closely is worth more than praise from anyone who hasn't.

Wednesday at eight your time. I'll send the link in the morning and there's nothing you need to bring.

## Why the age is named rather than implied

Fifty-five years of writing code is the thing that makes him unusual, and it is the thing he chose to tell. Naming it once, plainly, is respect; saying it three times would be flattery. The sentence also does the work of putting the owner's own experience in proportion without a word of self-deprecation.

## Why the praise is qualified

He corrected the register twice. That is the reason his approval counts, and saying so converts a compliment into a fact about him. Anybody can be encouraging; he was specific, twice, and the note records that instead of thanking him for warmth.

## Why nothing else is in it

No agenda, no questions, no price, no reference to Wednesday's content. Those go in the morning with the link, and mixing a thank-you with an ask would make the thank-you the opening move of a negotiation.
