## Reply, paste as is

Wanting it is the easy part. Getting it has usually taken a buyer rather than a supporter, because transparency arrives
when someone with a budget asks a question a prose record cannot answer, and this thread contains one: how often the
summary dropped the fact that decided the case.

The useful part of that question is that it needs nothing from the vendor. The buyer already holds the closed cases and
already employs the second reader, so the pressure needs no new powers and no cooperation from the supplier. It can be
run on a Tuesday by people who are already paid to read referrals.

What sits between wanting the record and having it is usually one procurement clause.

## What not to say

No thanks for the support, no restating the three moves he has already read, and no suggestion that regulators should act,
which is the answer that goes nowhere with the people who could actually ask. Nothing about the case study, no link, no
hashtag.

## Why the slot is worth it

He is the person in this thread whose audience can write that clause, and the reply hands him the one sentence that turns
a shared sentiment into a condition on a purchase. It also keeps the thread alive under a comment that is now carrying
the whole argument.
