# Thirty catalog buyers, each with a LinkedIn link I opened

Thirty named people. Every link below was fetched today, not copied from a search result. The fetch returns the person's headline, current company, company headcount, founding year, headquarters and employment history. Where a field is missing on the page it says "not stated" rather than a guess.

Companion file, same folder: `2026-11-13_catalog-buyers-30.csv` (same thirty rows, one row per person, importable).

## What this is not

It is not a list of Upwork buyers. That list cannot be built the way this one was built, and the reason matters more than the list.

A catalog buyer is someone who finds one of the three catalog items and pays through escrow. Until that person posts a job or opens a contract, Upwork shows nothing about them: no name, no employer, no link. There is no public page to verify against, because the buyer does not exist publicly until they act. Any "30 Upwork buyers with LinkedIn links" list is either scraped from unrelated job posts or invented.

A second constraint compounds it. Upwork forbids taking a contract off the platform and forbids exchanging contact details before one exists. So even a LinkedIn link collected legitimately could not be used to approach an Upwork-sourced buyer off-platform. That is a violation, not a shortcut.

What can be done, and is what this file is: thirty publicly named founders and engineering leads of small companies that have shipped or are shipping an LLM feature, each marked with the trigger that makes this week the week to speak to them, each reachable as a LinkedIn contact on LinkedIn's terms. The catalog remains the place money changes hands. This list is the top of the funnel that feeds it, and it is worked from this profile, not from an Upwork message.

## How the thirty were chosen

The trigger, from the 42-day ICP decision: founder or engineering lead at a 5 to 50 person company that has shipped or is about to ship an LLM feature into a product with paying customers, plus one dated event. The event is what makes the message timely: a funding round, a certification just completed or in progress, a launch, an enterprise pilot, a regulator.

The filter that removed most candidates: an agent that touches money, health data, legal advice, regulated lending or someone's credentials. A chatbot that answers FAQs over a marketing site does not have a buyer for this. An agent that posts to SAP or binds insurance does.

## How to work it

One message per person, from this profile, and the message carries no link and no offer. It asks a question that only someone with a live agent can answer, and it is written about their product, not ours.

The shape that has worked in this account before:

> Saw the Covera launch. Quoting across a hundred carrier portals means an agent that holds credentials and customer data in the same place. What is the plan for proving what it does when a carrier's security team asks, and is that written down yet?

Nothing else. No "I do this", no calendar link, no attachment. If they answer, the second message is one sentence about what a test produces. If they do not answer, the message was still about their problem, which is the only version of this that survives being read twice.

Order of work: rows 2, 5, 14, 16, 17, 20, 26, 29 first, in that order. Those eight have a dated trigger, a live regulated agent and fewer than fifty staff. Then 1, 9, 15, 19, 25. Then the rest, if the first thirteen produce any reply.

## What would make this list wrong

If eight of the first thirteen replies are "we already had an external review", the list is aimed at the wrong stage of company and should move to the pre-certification group. If the replies come and none convert, the problem is the offer, not the list. If nobody replies at all in ten days, the message is wrong, not the people, and the next version changes the question rather than the names.
