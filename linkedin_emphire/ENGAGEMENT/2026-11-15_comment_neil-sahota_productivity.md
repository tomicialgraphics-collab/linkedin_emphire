# Comment on Neil Sahota's productivity essay, 15 November 2026

What this is: one comment for his essay, aimed at the executives who read it rather than at the AI-strategy
peers who will agree with him in the replies. What to do next: paste it once and leave it. No link, no pitch.

## Why this angle and not agreement

Half his comment section will agree that attention is the constraint. Agreement is free and adds nothing, and
the piece already says it better than a comment can.

What the piece does not contain is the case where the externality can be measured and bought down. In security
work the review cost is the entire product: a generated finding that nobody can re-run is a claim the reviewer
has to carry, and one that arrives with a command and a pinned revision is a claim they can settle in a minute.
That is his argument with a mechanism attached, and it is the owner's actual job rather than an opinion about
his job.

The last line matters most. "Reviewable at what cost" is an entry he can put in a balance sheet he has already
proposed, which is worth more to a strategist than a compliment on the framework.

## Comment, paste this

The externality has a technical version worth pricing, because in engineering it can be bought down.

A security report is the clearest case. A model writes the findings in a minute and the person who has to act on
them spends the week working out which ones were real. That is the ten hours of review in your example, landing
on the person who never got the speed.

The counter-move is not less generation. It is making the output falsifiable. A finding that ships with a
command and a pinned revision costs the reviewer a rerun. The same finding as prose costs them a re-derivation,
and that gap is the whole externality.

I ran six injection attempts against my own chatbot and four worked. The number is worth nothing to a client
until they can run the six themselves and watch the same four fail. So the write-up carries the attempts, the
pinned revision and the rerun, and review becomes a rerun instead of an argument. In a regulated business that is
the difference between an audit response and a promise.

The line I would add to your balance sheet is reviewable at what cost. Anything that cannot be re-checked in one
command should be priced near zero.
