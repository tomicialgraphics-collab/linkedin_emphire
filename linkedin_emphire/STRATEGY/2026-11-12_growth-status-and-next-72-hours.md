# 5,000 by 25 December: where it stands on 12 November, and what the next 72 hours decide

The plan of 6 November still governs. This page reads it against the numbers that now exist, because on
6 November the single thing nobody could answer was whether 87 followers a day was a new baseline or a spike.
Six days later there is an answer, and it is not the one the plan hoped for.

## The arithmetic, with the current number

| | |
|---|---|
| Followers on 6 November | 1,007 |
| Followers on 12 November | 1,125 |
| Net new in six days | 118 |
| That rate | **19.7 a day** |
| Needed from today to 25 December | 3,875 over 43 days, **90.1 a day** |
| Projection at the current rate | about 1,970 |

The plan's Week 1 row expected 1,357 by tonight. The account is at 1,125, which is 232 behind the row and
about 4.6 times behind the daily rate the target requires. Run `./emphire growth` for this read-out on any
day; it reads `analytics/follower-log.md` and the checkpoints of 6 November, so the number cannot drift from
the plan without both saying so.

The 522 that arrived between 31 October and 6 November remains the only week in the record that ran at the
required pace. That week is the evidence that 87 a day is possible. It is not evidence that it is repeatable,
and the six days since are evidence that it did not repeat by itself.

## What that means, stated plainly

At 20 a day the target is arithmetically gone in the third week of December. It is not gone today. Ninety a
day for six weeks is what the target always required, and the only period in the record that hit it did so
while the account was doing one thing: writing in other people's threads every morning.

## The three moves that carry the number, in order

**1. Comment volume, on accounts bigger than this one, every weekday morning.** This is the lever that made
the first thousand and the only one that has ever run at the required rate. Five a day is the floor in the
6 November plan; the record that hit 87 a day was closer to eight or ten. The gate caps a comment at 1,250
characters and the writing standard is unchanged, so the constraint is supply of good source posts, not
wording. The daily 07:00 block exists for exactly this.

**2. One recurring series, so there is a reason to follow rather than a reason to like.** Single posts get
liked by people who already follow. A series gets followed by people who want part two. Three posts a week,
one of them always the series, same day, numbered. The material is already here: one metric or definition
problem taken apart each week.

**3. Profile conversion, checked once and then left alone.** Followers equal reach times profile visits
times follow rate. At this size the middle term is the cheapest to fix and the easiest to forget: the
headline, the first line of the about section, and the featured item. Two drafts for the first already exist
in `LINKEDIN/` and need an owner's decision, not another rewrite.

The fourth lever, borrowed audiences, has a lead time measured in weeks and should have been started on
6 November. It is now the only lever that can carry a large month inside the window, and it starts with one
ask this week, not five.

## The 15 November checkpoint, and why it matters more than usual

The plan set a decision for 15 November: under 1,600, the 5,000 target is arithmetically out of reach and the
honest choice is a stretch target of about 2,500 with the saved hours going into client work.

On today's pace the account arrives at 15 November near 1,184. That is 416 short of the floor with three days
to run, which needs 158 a day, and 158 a day has never happened in this record. So the checkpoint has already
answered itself, three days early, and pretending otherwise until the 15th spends three days on a number that
is decided.

What is worth doing in those three days is not chasing 1,600. It is finding out whether the comment lever
still works at volume: eight a day for three days, on accounts above 10,000 followers in lane, with the
follower line written each night. That is a test with an answer, and the answer sets the December target.

## What to stop

- **Posting more.** Two posts today, both good, and the second brought 42 impressions at forty-nine minutes
  while a comment written this morning sits in a thread that has been seen by more people than both posts.
- **News commentary.** The Nvidia story is the biggest thing in the feed and it is the most crowded. Two
  comments under it today were enough; a third adds an audience that reads news rather than hiring.
- **Anything bought.** Pods, follows, engagement groups. This was the owner's rule on 6 November and nothing
  has changed except that the shortfall now makes the temptation larger.

## The line to write tonight

`analytics/follower-log.md`, one row: date, followers, net, posts published, comments sent, best impression
count, what brought them. The table had a single baseline row for six days and now has a second. It is the
only instrument that can tell the difference between a bad week and a bad plan.
