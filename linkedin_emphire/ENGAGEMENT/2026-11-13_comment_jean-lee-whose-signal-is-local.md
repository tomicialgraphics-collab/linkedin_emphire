## Comment, paste as is

The bet underneath this one is not the cold application. It is that you had a reason to distrust your own circle's
read of the company. Your circle could see KakaoTalk at home, and you were watching adoption in Europe and India, so
you were holding two pieces of evidence and only one of them was available to the people telling you to look elsewhere.

That is the part I would pull out for anyone job hunting now. Applying cold is the cheap move and anyone can make it.
Noticing when your network's opinion is built on local visibility is the harder one, and almost every market has a
version of it: a product that is obvious from somewhere else and invisible from where you are standing.

Nineteenth engineer is a different bet as well. At that size the work you do is legible to the people who decide
whether it was good, which is the thing you said you went looking for at IBM and did not find.

## What not to say

No cold application story of my own, no praise of the writing, and no advice about job hunting that she has already
given in better words. No reference to the acquisition as a lesson, since she is explicit that she did not know about
it and the story is better without the hindsight bolted on.

## Why the slot is worth it

Her audience is engineers deciding where to work next, and the useful reframe is that the bet was about evidence
rather than courage. It is a comment a reader can act on this week, which is what makes it worth posting under a story
that will otherwise collect a hundred versions of the same encouragement.
