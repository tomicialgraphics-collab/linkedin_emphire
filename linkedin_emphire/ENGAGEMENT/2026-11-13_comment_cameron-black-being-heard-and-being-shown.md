## Comment, paste as is

Being allowed to raise it and being allowed to show it are two different permissions, and the second is the one under pressure here. A researcher can pass every test of psychological safety, say the difficult thing in the room, and still watch the paper sit in review for months. The concern was heard. The evidence stayed inside, which to everyone outside the building is the same outcome as never raising it.

For a funder that is the part with teeth. A grant condition that fixes publication rights, with a clock on the review, does what a whistleblowing policy cannot, because the person raising the concern no longer has to be brave to make the finding visible.

The 3 November report rests on current and former staff speaking anonymously, so the firm version is that review has tightened and papers have stalled, not that a named finding was suppressed. That is still enough to make the contract question worth asking.

No speculation about what Google actually suppressed, because the sourcing will not carry it and he is a researcher who will check.
