Twelve-day post run

Written 2 November 2026. Covers Monday 2 November to Friday 13 November, which is the day before the follow-up goes out on the 14th.

The premise

If somebody at a research foundation opens your profile after an application or a message, they are not reading your posts. They are scanning for one thing: does this person treat a measurement as an instrument that has to be calibrated, or as a number that has to be announced. Everything in this run is built to answer that in the first two posts and to keep answering it down the page.

Themes are aligned to how that kind of organisation thinks. Nothing in the run names them, tags them, quotes them or alludes to them. There is no fourth pillar, no borrowed phrase, no "I have been following the work of a certain Amsterdam foundation". It reads as a person with a consistent position, which is the only version of this that survives being read closely.

Why this order

Posts two and four are the argument. Posts one, three and five are the evidence that the argument is not decorative. Post six is the one that cannot be faked: naming eight defects in your own benchmark, with ids, is a thing people do only when they expect to be checked.

If somebody reads only the top of the profile they see the newest post, which is why the open-benchmark one goes last. If they read three, they get the defects, the judge and the rubric, which is the whole position. If they read all six, the order steps from a public number, to values, to compliance, to mechanism, to the judge, to the ledger.

If twelve days is too long

Four posts is enough: 2, 4, 11, 13. That is the denominator, the pluralism, the judge and the ledger. Drop #17 and #16 without losing anything structural; they are the ones that add texture rather than argument.

Do not compress the other four into a single week. Two of them on consecutive mornings looks like a campaign, and one every other day looks like a practice.

The rules for the whole window

  1. Their name does not appear in any post, comment, hashtag, image or reply. Not once in twelve days.
  2. No tagging them again. The post on 23 October already cited them openly and politely; repeating it inside the application window changes it from citing work to courting a reader.
  3. No replies in their comment sections that restate a post of yours or point at the profile. If you comment on their posts this fortnight, comment on the substance and keep it shorter than theirs.
  4. No mention of the application, the role, the take-home or the interview, anywhere, in any comment.
  5. Do not claim any relationship. No "in conversation with", no "reviewing with", no plural we.
  6. One comment per post, your own, posted within a minute of publishing. No engagement-bait openers, no polls, no "agree?".
  7. If a post lands badly, do not delete it and do not explain it. Answer the strongest objection once, in public, and leave it up.

What they will look at, in order

  1. The newest post. That is #21 if the grid holds.
  2. The headline, if the posts did their job. If the headline does not say what you build, the run is carrying the headline's weight for it. Send me the current one and I will draft three alternatives.
  3. The last four posts, scrolling. That is #21, #20, #16, #17 in feed order, which is a coherent paragraph on its own.
  4. Whether the numbers you cite are dated and sourced. Every post in this run that uses a figure has it in a sources.md, and the two new ones state what they do not claim.

After the 14th

The follow-up goes out once, on the 14th, per AITHOS-PREP.md §10. If they reply, no further posts are needed and the run has done its job. If they do not, the run stays up and keeps working as a portfolio: nothing about it expires on the 15th.
