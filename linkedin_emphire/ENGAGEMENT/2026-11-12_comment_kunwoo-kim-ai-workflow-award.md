## Comment, paste as is

I don't run a firm, so I can't answer your closing question from the inside. What I can say is that I test systems other people have already built and called done, and scalability is the criterion I'd watch hardest in that programme.

The failure I meet most often is a working tool in the wrong hands. It passes its own tests on the builder's machine, and nobody outside that team can invoke it. Real feature, no reach.

That maps onto a workflow award almost exactly. What matters is whether a colleague in another office, working only from what the associate left behind, ends up with the same result. Where the answer is yes, the firm has bought a change. Where it's no, it has bought a good account of one.

Which is why the hours credit is the version I'd copy first. It spends time the firm was already buying, and it produces the evidence a prize then has to judge. Money for a story about a change is the more expensive way to learn the same thing.

One addition to the nomination form, if they want it: hand the write-up to an associate who has never spoken to the author, give them an hour, and see whether they finish in the same place.
