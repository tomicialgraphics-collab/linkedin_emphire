# Your traffic points, and why impressions are not one of them

## The two answers, before the evidence

**Where the traffic is.** Four metrics, one page, `upwork.com/nx/my-stats/`: profile views, invitations, proposals (sent, viewed, interviews, hires), and impressions and clicks. Earnings, Connects and badge status sit on the same page. That page is login-only, so I can name the metrics and hand you the link, not read your numbers.

**Does more impressions raise visibility.** No. Impressions count how often a **boosted** profile was shown. They are the meter on paid placement, not a ranking input. With Boost off, that number cannot move at all, so there is nothing to grow and nothing to lose by not growing it.

## The definition that settles it

Upwork's own article, fetched at 11:46 WAT today, defines each metric in one line. This is the whole dispute in four rows.

| label on your page | Upwork's words |
|---|---|
| Profile views | "How many people checked out your profile" |
| Invitations | "How many invites you've received" |
| Impressions and clicks | "**(when boosted)** How often your boosted profile showed up and how many people clicked" |
| Proposals | "It also shows whether the proposals, views, and responses were boosted or organic." |

The parenthesis is Upwork's, not mine. Impressions are not visits and not viewers. They are surfacings inside a placement you paid Connects for. A click is a client opening the card from that placement; the community threads are consistent that repeat clicks by the same client count once.

Two consequences follow, and they are the answer to your second question.

1. **Impressions are downstream of Connects.** You buy the placement, then the counter records that the placement happened. Spending more raises the number. A higher number does not raise the placement.
2. **Boost off means the number is flat by design.** Not slow, not unlucky, not a sign the profile is hidden. It is a meter on a circuit that is switched off.

There is no organic impressions figure on Upwork at all. The analogous numbers are profile views and invitations, and both are already listed separately.

## Where impressions sit, and do not sit, in Upwork's ranking

Upwork publishes what its search ranking takes into account. Verbatim from the search-features page:

- "Freelancer profile relevance to the search query"
- "Category-based hiring patterns"
- "Specific client account activities, including previous searches, as well as active and previous open jobs"
- "Client intent, like browsing versus actively searching to fill an open job"
- "Client price preferences"

Every item is either about how well your profile matches the query or about the client's own behaviour. Impressions and clicks appear nowhere. They are not a criterion. They are a receipt.

Two more official statements point the same way, from Upwork's marketing guide for freelancers:

- "Freelancers with a higher JSS will appear in search results ahead of those who have lower scores."
- "Clients can also filter by talent badge when searching for independent professionals."

And on the overview: "only the first 250 or so will show up in client search results."

None of those three can be bought with a boost. The JSS one cannot be bought at all.

## What actually raises the number you care about

"Visibility" for you means profile views and invitations, and both come off the same card.

1. **Proposals.** The only channel you fully control, and every one puts your profile in front of a client who is hiring this week. It is also what makes the stats page populate in the first place; Upwork says the page starts after the first proposal or direct hire.
2. **The first closed contract with feedback.** This is the structural change. JSS appears, badge eligibility opens at full completeness plus 90% JSS, and every card you own in search gains the two things the three engineers above you at 11:42 already had. Until then you compete on rate alone, which is exactly what that search showed.
3. **The relevance half is already done.** Five portfolio items and twenty skills carrying the demand phrases. That work is finished; it is no longer the constraint.
4. **Availability badge, decided separately.** It displays in results and costs Connects per week. Both of your near-competitors show "Available now". I am not folding it into this answer because it is a spend decision, not a visibility fact.
5. **Boost last, if at all.** Boosting now buys impressions and clicks into a card that still has no badge and no score, which is the comparison a client makes in the first second.

## What is not official, so do not manage to it

Third-party guides assert a keyword weighting, a 90% JSS advantage, an 80% suppression floor, a proposal-to-interview penalty and a 60 to 90 day freshness signal. Upwork publishes none of those thresholds. They happen to point the same direction as the official advice, so they are useful as folklore and useless as targets. Do not chase a number nobody has confirmed exists.

## What I need from you for actual figures

Stats and trends, 7 day and 30 day, four sections: Profile metrics, Proposals, Earnings, Badge. Include the timestamp at the top, because Upwork says the label beside profile views ("25 days ago") is when the last view happened, not when the data refreshed. A blank section is also a finding: it means not enough work history yet, not that nothing is happening.

One correction to hold. The 11,144 impressions figure in your notes is LinkedIn's. Upwork has no impressions number for you while Boost is off.

Impressions are a receipt for Connects, not a lever on visibility.
