## Comment, paste this

The part I'd underline is the refinancing wall, because what gets lent against is utilisation, and utilisation
mostly comes out of the borrower's own telemetry.

Hours rented, tokens served, capacity committed. A GPU-backed loan is underwritten on numbers produced by the
operator being financed, and the covenants tend to check the reporting rather than the reality. The same shape
shows up a layer up: the productivity claims that justify the spend are self-reported by the teams that bought the
tools, and nobody re-runs them.

That's a quieter difference from 1999 than the debt is. Fibre sat in the ground and you could count the
strands. This buildout's economics depend on a figure the seller produces and the buyer repeats, and a number
nobody outside the loop has verified behaves like a number that is fine until it isn't.

If I were doing diligence, I'd ask who signs the utilisation figure, and what would have to happen for that
person to be shown wrong. Most of these structures don't have an answer.
