Of the four, I meet the second and third most often, and they fail in the same place. Mapping obligations to controls is visible work, so it gets funded and it gets finished. The evidence step underneath is where it stops: a check reports pass because it never ran, and on a dashboard "no findings" and "didn't run" look identical. The opportunity I would rank first is duller than the other three. Each control emits a dated artifact at the moment it executes, and something confirms the artifact exists before that pass is allowed to count. That is what makes an evidence chain evidence instead of a folder of documents. It also costs less than another mapping layer.
