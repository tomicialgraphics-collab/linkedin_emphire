The day someone asks to be forgotten, the record of what you did becomes the problem.

Every system I have been trusted to check keeps some version of that record: what was registered, by whom, on what date. The record is usually the product. It is also a line about a person.

Then a deletion request arrives. You can delete the file. The line saying the file existed on that date, and that a named party registered it, is the one you cannot delete without destroying the only evidence that anything happened.

I had to choose that this week, on a record I am building. It keeps 4 things per registration: the file, its fingerprint, who registered it, and the date. None of them says anything about whether the paper is true. All 4 of them are about somebody.

There are two honest answers. Keep the name, and say in the sentence people read before they upload, not in a policy page nobody opens, that the note survives deletion. Or keep the record and drop the person, so what is left is a link number and a date, and you can say when something was opened without ever knowing who held the phone.

Most teams pick neither. They store everything, promise deletion in the terms, and meet the contradiction when a request lands with a deadline attached.

So the question is not whether you can delete it. It is whether you can still prove what you did afterwards.

## First comment, within twenty minutes

The distinction I keep coming back to: a record can be true about an event and useless about a person, or useful about a person and dangerous to keep. Both of those are design decisions, and neither one is a policy.

If you have already had an erasure request land on something you built to be evidence, I would like to hear what you deleted and what you refused.

## Why this one

The vault work this week forced the choice into the open. Writing it as a public argument does two things: it tests the reasoning in front of people who do this for a living, and it puts the honest version of the product claim somewhere a future buyer can find it.
