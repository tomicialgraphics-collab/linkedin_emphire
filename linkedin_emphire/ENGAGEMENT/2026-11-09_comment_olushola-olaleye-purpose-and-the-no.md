## The angle

Take his premise seriously and then supply the mechanism it leaves out. Purpose as a standard works fine while the
opportunity is hypothetical. It fails in the room, under time pressure, in front of a team, which is where the decision is
actually made. The fix is a written rule made in the quiet, because a rule survives an offer at eleven at night and a
conviction being rebuilt on the spot usually does not.

Then the part that keeps people honest: the nos are invisible to everyone except the person saying them.

## Comment, paste as is

The hardest ones to refuse aren't the bad ideas. They're good money with the wrong shape, and a flattering invitation from someone you'd like to impress. Those arrive as decisions in a room, under pressure, with your team watching, and purpose is not much help in that room.

What helps is a rule you wrote down while the offer was still hypothetical. A rule you can check against survives an email at eleven at night. A conviction you're reconstructing on the spot usually doesn't.

The other part nobody says out loud: the nos are invisible. People see what you shipped, never the work that got the time instead. That is why this discipline goes unrewarded in public, and why most people quietly drift back.
