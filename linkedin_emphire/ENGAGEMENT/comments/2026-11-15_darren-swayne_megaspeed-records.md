# Comment on Darren Swayne's Nvidia and Megaspeed post

<!-- gate:body:start -->
The detail that will matter in a hearing is not the $12bn. It is the partner list.

Preferred allocation ran through Nvidia's cloud partner programme, so each of those shipments traces back to a decision about who got scarce chips first. That list is a governance artefact, it sat in the company's own systems the whole time, and nobody was obliged to reconcile it against end-user declarations.

A GPU is also one of the few objects in a supply chain that names itself when it boots, through its driver, its management plane and the billing record of whoever is renting it. So the trail existed at both ends: who was allocated, and where the hardware turned up. Joining those two datasets was simply optional.

Which is the reusable part for anyone shipping AI. The record you want at incident review has to be the record you chose to write at decision time.

The 100,000 signatures measure attention. Allocation records are what a regulator reads.
