# Comment on Andreas Horn's Nvidia security post, 8 November 2026

## Why this angle

He has made the strategic argument well and it does not need agreeing with. The addition has to be the part his argument implies
but does not say: access is people-shaped all the way down, and the person worth recruiting is usually not the chief executive.

Second reason to avoid the obvious: the same story was in this feed earlier from a governance angle, and that comment went at
the board statement's structure. Repeating a neighbouring point under a different post is how a feed starts to look like one
note played on several instruments.

## Comment

The uncomfortable extension is that it is not only the leaders. Access lives in people all the way down, and the person worth
recruiting is rarely the chief executive. It is whoever can approve a deploy, flip a flag, or hand over a token that already
carries the permissions.

Which is an argument for the boring controls, and for one most teams have not done: knowing what a single compromised account
can reach. Agents make that harder rather than easier, because authority now gets delegated to non-human identities. Nobody
recruited them, so nobody watches them the way they watch staff.

You cannot train your way out of that. You can only constrain what one person's fall gets you.

## What not to say

No speculation about the investigation or about the woman named in the reporting. No opinion on whether the espionage element
is true, since he has already flagged it as unproven and that is the correct handling. No mention of my own work and no link,
under a post that ends with a newsletter call.

## Held for a later turn

If he replies, the real conversation is agent identity: what a non-human identity should be allowed to inherit from the
person who created it, and whether an agent should ever hold standing authority rather than authority scoped to one task.
That is squarely his advisory territory and squarely the argument this account keeps making from the evidence side.
