## Comment, paste as is

Your reframing earns its keep on the fifth question, and that's the one I'd push hardest.

Supplier power for a professional used to come from holding scarce knowledge. Increasingly it comes from holding access: a licence, a toolchain, a model nobody else in the room can reach. That reads on a CV exactly like the old thing and behaves like its opposite. Access is granted, priced and withdrawn by somebody else, so a diagnosis that counts it as an asset will score dependency as strength.

One more thing belongs in the instrument itself. A question nobody can fail is not a diagnosis, it's a mood. For each of your five, the useful follow-up is what evidence would change the answer. For substitutes that's concrete: which tasks did a machine finish last quarter, and what did the invoice say. For the buyer question, it's who else was asked to quote.

Most people answer all five from memory, which means the instrument measures confidence rather than position.

Which may be the gap you're pointing at in AI education. Not whether someone uses the tools well, but whether they can produce one claim about their own position that somebody else can check.

## Why the fifth force

Four of his questions ask the individual to estimate something about themselves and their field. His fifth asks about dependence on specific technologies and information, and that is the only one where the answer changed materially in the last two years, because access to a model became the thing people mistake for expertise.

That distinction matters for his audience. HRD teams will build an assessment out of these five, and an assessment whose answers arrive as self-report measures the confident, not the exposed. It offers the correction without attacking the framework.

## What this is not

This is not a pitch, not a link, and not a correction of Porter. It takes his reframing as given and adds one thing to the instrument.

Nor a claim to know the Korean market. The comment is written so it would be true in any market where access is licensed rather than owned.

## If he replies

A question worth having is whether he has seen an organisation score people on the fifth force and act on the result, because most stop at the assessment and never touch the dependency it reveals. That is his subject and an honest answer would be useful to him.
