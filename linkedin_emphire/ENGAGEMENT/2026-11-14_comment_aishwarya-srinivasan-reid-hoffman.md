The line worth pulling out is yours, not his: that how someone approaches the questions is the lesson, rather than any answer in the interview.

That's more testable than it sounds. Watch what a person asks for before they believe a number. The operators and investors I learn most from don't ask what the model does, they ask who measured it and whether they could rerun it. That one question clears most of a deck.

The tell is the same in a pitch as in a research talk. The person who can say what would have made them wrong is the one who has actually looked, and the one who can't is usually quoting someone else's result.
