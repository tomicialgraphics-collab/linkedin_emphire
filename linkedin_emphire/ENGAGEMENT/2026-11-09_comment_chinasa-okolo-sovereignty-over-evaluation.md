## The angle

Take the one instrument in her list that has teeth, which is data licensing, and name what sits next to it. A licence governs
the corpus. It does not govern the benchmarks, so the systems built on licensed data are still judged by test sets written
elsewhere, in another language and against another country's failure modes.

That gives one honest addition in his own lane: evaluation is the part of sovereignty that has to be built locally, and a small
suite a ministry or a five person team can run alone is what converts the claim into a control.

## Comment, paste as is

The data licensing models are the part I keep thinking about, and the gap I would name beside them is test sets. A licence protects the corpus, but the systems built on it will still be measured by benchmarks written elsewhere, in someone else's language and someone else's failure modes. Sovereignty over the data is real. Sovereignty over how the system gets judged is the part still on the table.

A regional eval suite that a ministry or a five person team can run on its own is what turns that claim into a control.
