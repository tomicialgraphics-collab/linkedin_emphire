## Reply, paste as is

Read the lineage and the persistence record, and the part I keep coming back to is BS-003 and BS-004 staying failed in public. Keeping a failed predecessor where it landed is rarer than any pass.

The probe I would add is the one the pair as published does not cover: age rather than change. Same packet, same values, same high-consequence class, but the authority evidence two hours past its freshness window instead of one state transition behind. A path can pass your transition test and still act on a stale assertion, and the only thing that catches it is a trace naming the timestamp it judged, and refusing on the age rather than on the value.

If a BS-006 ever gets constituted, that is the specimen I would want to watch.

## Why this reply and not the obvious one

An obvious reply is a second round of agreement, and it adds nothing to a thread where we already agree twice. A second obvious reply is to look for the flaw, and the page has been built to survive most versions of that: frozen criteria declared before the run, the failed predecessors left in place, the boundary of the claim written out, and the artefacts a stranger can check, including a manifest and the primitive hashes.

So the reply takes the record seriously and adds the specimen the family does not contain. Both published specimens move one variable, the material state transition, and both were evaluated twenty five seconds apart on the same runtime and governance pack. That is a clean pair for change. Nothing in the pair tests age, which is the other way authority evidence fails: the values stay the same and the evidence goes stale on the shelf. A path that refuses on a changed binding can still admit an unchanged binding whose witness is hours old, and the difference is only visible if the trace names the timestamp it judged.

His version labels suggest the machinery exists, since authority continuity is at 1.9.1 with a provenance freshness witness in the name. Whether a specimen has exercised it is a different question from whether the primitive exists, and that is the question the reply asks.

## Two things this reply does not do

It does not claim the lineage is unverifiable. Their page publishes a manifest and the persistence record carries request and trace ids, primitive versions and per specimen digests, which is far more than most vendors put in public. My own hash check is the thing that failed, not their record, and the front matter says so.

It does not restate the provenance point he already conceded. He agreed that the agent's account is not a substitute for the enforcing receiver's records, and repeating an agreement is how a thread becomes a mutual admiration exercise.
