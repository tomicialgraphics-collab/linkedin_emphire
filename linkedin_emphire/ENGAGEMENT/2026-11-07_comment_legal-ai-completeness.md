# Comment on the legal AI accuracy-versus-completeness post, 7 November 2026

## What the record says

The post distinguishes verification, which asks whether the propositions presented are correct, from
completeness, which asks whether the material issues necessary for a defensible conclusion were
investigated. It argues legal AI is being measured only on the first, and that the more useful capability is
a system that surfaces the questions a practitioner did not think to ask.

The distinction is sound and does not need defending. What the post does not supply is the artefact that
would make completeness checkable, and that is the contribution.

## Draft, paste as a comment

This distinction is the right one, and it gets testable the moment you ask for the paths the system closed,
not just the one it took.

Accuracy produces true statements. Completeness needs evidence of the search: which conditions were tested,
which failed, which were never reached. In most output those three states look identical, because a
provision that was considered and dismissed and one that was never opened both leave silence.

Legal file notes already solve this. They record what was considered and rejected, and that is what makes
the advice defensible two years later. AI output usually keeps the conclusion and drops the search.

So the evaluation question shifts: can the record show the questions that were asked, and the ones that
were not.

## Why this shape

- Agrees once and moves on. He has made the argument well and a second version of it would be padding.
- The mechanism is the value: a dismissed provision and an unconsidered provision are indistinguishable in
  normal output, which is exactly why completeness cannot be scored from an answer.
- The file-note comparison is in his own professional language. The artefact already exists in legal
  practice, so the point is that AI output is missing it rather than that law needs a new idea.
