# Comment on Mourad Elmaaloui's sovereign AI post, 8 November 2026

## Why this angle

His framing is good, the three-way split is genuinely useful, and the deals are real. So the comment cannot be a better version
of his own structure. It has to add the layer his three models all leave out, and there is a clean one: none of the three buys the
ability to judge whether the output is good.

Model 2 is the sharpest place to make it, because Frontier is a platform that carries evaluation inside it, which means the
national layer sits underneath a judgement layer somebody else operates.

## Comment

Model 2 is the one to watch, and it shows the layer all three models leave out: whoever supplies the evaluation.

ADNOC keeps the data, the compute is G42's, and the platform underneath holds the evaluation that decides whether an agent's
output is good enough to act on. That works, and it means the judgement about quality lives one layer above the infrastructure
the country just bought. Sovereign compute with borrowed benchmarks is still borrowing the part that says yes.

The cheapest sovereignty on that list is the one nobody is buying yet: a domestically held set of test cases, with the version
history of every change made to them.

## What not to say

No recital of his three models, no opinion on which will win by 2030, since his question is a forecast and a comment is not the
place for a guess presented as analysis. No Qatar figures, which were not independently checked, and no speculation about the
deals' commercial terms.

## Held for a later turn

If he replies, the conversation worth having is whether a national evaluation set is realistic when the models change every few
months, since a benchmark held domestically goes stale faster than the infrastructure it tests. That is a real objection and it
is better as a discussion than as a caveat in a comment.
