# Two connection requests, 6 November

## David Moskowitz MD, GenoMed

**Verdict: ignore. Do not accept, do not reply.**

He is not a crank in the sense of being nobody. He is a nephrologist with a real company, a real
published paper and a claim he has repeated for thirty-one years. That is exactly why this is a
decision rather than a reflex.

Three reasons to leave it.

1. What he wants is reach, not a conversation. His message names no question, asks for nothing he
   could get from a 6 November connection, and closes with references available upon request. The ask
   is amplification, and it arrives in the first message.
2. The framing hardened long ago and it is not the owner's. Dialysis as a Holocaust, healthcare as refusing to
   listen: thirty-one years of that has hardened into a position that a new contact cannot move and
   will be asked to repeat. Accepting makes the owner a carrier of it.
3. The lane pays for it. He is building a reputation for evidence in AI evaluation. A connection to a
   claim this large, held this long, with a listed company behind it, is a question he would have to
   answer at some point, and the answer is not his to give.

One thing to know if he writes again and the question becomes direct. ACE inhibitors and ARBs are
already standard of care in proteinuric chronic kidney disease, so the mainstream did not ignore the
mechanism. The extraordinary part of his claim is reversal, and 90 percent prevention, and the total
silence of every health system since 1994. That is the part that needs the evidence, and it is the
part a connection request cannot supply.

What would change the verdict: a message that asks for a specific piece of verification rather than
for reach, and states what result would falsify the claim. Nothing in this message does.
