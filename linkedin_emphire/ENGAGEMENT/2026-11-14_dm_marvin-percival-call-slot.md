## Why each line is there

**The day, named.** He asked for a day and offered a window. Answering with a time and a number of minutes closes the loop; answering with "any day works" hands the work back to him.

**"As you had it."** His conversion is correct, so the reply agrees with his arithmetic rather than re-explaining the clock. Agreement on a small fact is cheap and it buys the harder sentence later in the message.

**The licence line, placed third.** "It is not my content" is true and irrelevant: the licence field is a claim made by whoever published the card. Putting that third, after two items he can act on, keeps the call booked. Putting it first would have made the message an argument, and an argument is what a person cancels.

**"I'd rather hear how you see it than guess."** It states the objection and hands him the floor in the same clause. He can agree, disagree, or explain, and all three keep the call.
