# Comment on Sam Illingworth's AI feedback post, 14 November 2026

## What the post says, and what the paper adds

The post reports an eight week study where each student got one style of AI comment: correct the work, suggest a strategy, prompt self checking, or encourage. The encouraging group kept returning and did not improve. Sam's line is that usage can be bought with synthetic warmth, and that usage is the headline number in almost every education AI pitch.

All of that holds against the paper. What the paper adds is the pricing. Three mediators carry three different outcomes, and the abstract gives each one a share: competence 38 percent of the feedback to achievement relationship, autonomy 34 percent of the feedback to engagement relationship, relatedness 22 percent of the feedback to persistence relationship. Self level feedback strengthened relatedness uniquely, which is why that arm's only live path ran to persistence.

So the study does not just show a group that came back without improving. It shows which of the three paths the returning was, and that the other two were still, which is a stronger and more usable claim than warmth versus learning.

## Why that is worth a comment rather than a repetition

It converts the finding into something a product team can check in its own data. If engagement and learning are separately instrumented, a team can say which of the three mediators a cohort moved and by how much. If the dashboard has one field for a session and none for the need it met, then the retention chart is the 22 percent path with a headline on it, and the 38 percent path is not measured rather than measured low.

That is the same defect as a verifier that exits 0 on NOT TESTED, wearing a different hat: one number standing in for states it cannot distinguish. It is also the one thing in this thread that a reader can go and do on Monday.

## Comment to paste

Sam, this study hands your readers a sharper test than the headline, and the abstract already prices it.

The three mediators carry different outcomes. Competence accounts for 38% of the feedback effect on achievement, autonomy 34% on engagement, relatedness 22% on persistence. Self-level feedback, the encouraging kind, strengthened relatedness uniquely. So the group that kept coming back was moving the 22% path while the 38% path sat still. That is a better statement than warmth versus learning, because the paper tells you which path each design loads.

It also makes the claim auditable. Any team shipping a tutor can ask which of the three mediators its sessions are moving, and whether the telemetry can tell. The ones I look at usually have one field for a session and none for the need it met, so retention is the 22% path with a headline on it, and achievement is unmeasured rather than low.

At which point the useful thing to tell a buyer is not that engagement is real. It is that engagement and learning are instrumented separately, and here is the week they diverged.
