I read it properly rather than from the card alone, and it is worse than the link suggested.

Five sizes. The overview says 16 GB. The statistics table says 95 GB. The file listing says 95 GB. The dataset metadata says under 1K. The viewer says one row. Those are not roundings of each other, they are five answers to one question, and only the fifth is consistent with the 394 valid JSON files out of 507 that the same card reports.

Two files are flagged unsafe by Hugging Face's own scanner. The card does not say which two or what tripped it. That banner is the only sentence on the page written by the platform rather than the uploader, and anyone who clones the repository wholesale inherits whatever it caught.

Apache 2.0 over content described as taken from HackTheBox, TryHackMe, PortSwigger Academy, SANS and VulnHub. None of those licence redistribution of their material, so the grant is not the uploader's to make. Nine models are fine-tuned on it and it pulled 20,702 downloads last month, so the problem is already travelling.

PhD-level is a string inside the records. The task type is literally phd_level_reference, which is a field, not a standard.

I only read the page, and I have not opened a single file, so I am making no claim about whether the content is good. What I am claiming is narrower. A page that cannot state its own size in one number has not been checked by the person who wrote it, and that is usually the first symptom rather than the disease.

The useful thing for your call is not this dataset. It is that provenance, licence and integrity were checkable in ten minutes from a public page, and nobody in those 20,702 downloads appears to have checked.

Send the windows when you have them.
