## What the answer has to do

Say yes, define the version he can actually run, define the version he cannot, and offer the protocol. Anything vaguer reads as
talk. Anything larger is a promise he cannot keep, which is worse than a no.

The professional detail is the scoring: a detection test has to count false alarms as well as hits, because a participant who flags
everything is not vigilant, and most designs reward exactly that.

## Reply, paste as is

Yes, but as a smaller instrument than the one I described, and labelled as such.

What I can run without an institution is a detection test rather than a learning study: twenty to forty people, a fixed task set, outputs silently corrupted at a known rate, scored on hits and false alarms separately. Flagging everything must not score as vigilance, which is where a lot of these designs fall over.

What that cannot tell you is whether training builds the skill. That needs your randomised design, your sample size and an ethics board.

If the protocol is useful to you, I'll write it up and send it.
