## Comment, paste as is

The step I would pick is smaller than it sounds: test one control somewhere real, then write down what you found, with the evidence you looked at and one thing you could not check.

A certificate says you sat an exam, and a hiring manager can't do much with that. What they can do is read a work sample and decide whether the person could hold a conversation with their auditor. Something like: I checked MFA on the finance system, most accounts had it on, two were exceptions with no documented approval, and I couldn't see the ticketing system so I can't tell whether they were waived. That does more for a junior application than a year of posting about learning.

The line people leave out is the limitation, and it is the part that makes the rest believable. Anyone can report that a control passed. Saying what you couldn't check is what an assessor does on their first day.

Find an organisation small enough to say yes, take the names out, and publish it imperfect.

## Why this answer

He asks for one step, so the comment gives one and makes it small enough to do this week. That step is a control test with a written result, because that is the only artefact in the list he could name that a GRC hiring manager is able to assess. A post about learning shows interest. A certificate shows attendance. A two paragraph test write-up shows whether the person can look at evidence and reach a conclusion, which is the job.

There is a mild disagreement in the middle of the comment and it is deliberate. His second bullet is to share what you are learning publicly, and the honest version for this field is that the sharing is worth much less than the artefact. Saying it plainly, once, without contradicting the rest of his advice, is more useful to his readers than agreeing with all of it.

Its limitation line is the part I would keep. It is what separates a claim from an assessment, and it is the first thing an experienced reviewer looks for, because anybody can report a pass.

An easy comment here is encouragement, or a story about how I started, and both would be about the commenter. This audience has no use for either. The other temptation is to mention the playbook or a course, which would make the comment an advert under a post by somebody who sells training, and that is the worst possible place for one.
