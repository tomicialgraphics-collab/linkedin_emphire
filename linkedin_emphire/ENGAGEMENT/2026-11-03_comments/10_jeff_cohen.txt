The admission that manual evaluation is what broke the company is the most useful line in this post, and it is not only a speed problem. A reviewer's verdict in a spreadsheet leaves no artifact, so six weeks later nobody can tell whether the agent changed or the reviewer did. A guardrail that records only pass and fail keeps that blindness and removes the latency. What holds up under audit is the version where every verdict carries its input, its rubric and its model version, so the next person can re-derive the decision instead of trusting it. "The model did it" and "the reviewer said so" fail the same test.
