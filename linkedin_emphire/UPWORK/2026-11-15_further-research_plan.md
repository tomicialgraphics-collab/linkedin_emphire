# Further research: what is worth doing, and what is not

Forty days to 25 December. Research that does not change a decision in that window is a way of feeling busy. So the
list below is sorted by what it would change, not by how interesting it is.

## The three that change a decision this week

**1. The demand read on the job feed.** Four searches (`AI red team`, `AI model evaluation`, `MCP server`,
`Claude Code`), and for each post record five things: proposals, budget, whether the client has spent before, how many
interviewing, and whether the post asks for a *report* or a *fix*. Twenty minutes in your own browser, because the
counts sit behind your login and not mine. This settles the question the whole lane pick rests on: does anyone actually
buy red-team work on this platform, or only write about wanting it? It also gives the realistic range for the $1,200
first milestone. Nothing else on this list is close to as valuable.

**2. A portfolio teardown of the five profiles at or above your price.** Don D. at $95, Ahmad A. at $85.55, Orest A.
at $85, Elena R. at $65, Guy P. at $65. For each: how many portfolio items, what is inside the first three, is there a
named deliverable or only a service list, and does the card show a price. This is public, so I can do it without you.
It answers one question precisely: what does credibility look like at $65, counted rather than guessed?

**3. A corpus of thirty real posts in the two lanes.** Public postings are indexed by aggregators even when the live
page wants a login. Pull thirty across red teaming and evals, then code them: budget, duration, skills named, whether
they mention OWASP or garak or PyRIT, and what the deliverable is. Roughly two hours of my time. It tells you what the
buyer's words are, which is the difference between a first line that survives screening and one that does not.

## The four worth running next week, in parallel

| study | question it settles | who | cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fee and Connects schedule | what $1,200 actually pays after fees, and what a send costs | me, public page | 15 minutes, and it closes an open tracker item that has sat since 11 October |
| December seasonality | whether Q4 goes quiet, which decides push-now or hold-for-January | me, Upwork monthly reports plus post volumes | 45 minutes |
| Your own send log | the only real conversion data you will ever have: sends, views, replies, by lane and by price | you, a four-column log | 5 minutes per send, forever |
| First-review economics | how many jobs a badge needs and what one review does to placement | me, Upwork support pages plus the tracker | 30 minutes, likely low yield |

## The public-portfolio audit is bigger than it looks

Worth saying plainly, because it is the part I can carry alone. For each of the five competitors I can record: item
count, whether the items are case studies or screenshots, whether any shows a rerunnable artefact, the first sentence
of the overview, and the consultation price if there is one. That produces a table you can hold your own card against,
and it costs you nothing but reading it.

## Seven I would refuse, with reasons

| study | why not |
|---|---|
| Market sizing for AI red teaming | analyst totals do not change what you charge one client, and the numbers disagree by an order of magnitude |
| Scraping rates at scale | the pager caps at 10,001 and the counts are word-matches; entry 27 closed this |
| Talent-search counts, any variant | the same cap, the same noise; composition only |
| LinkedIn demand signals | a different buyer with a different buying process, and your pipeline is Upwork plus the HFB pack |
| Pricing psychology literature | you need three sales, not a theory of anchoring |
| Competitor content audits | their posts do not tell you what converts; only your sends do |
| More keyword matrices | the matrix exists and the box does not honour it; entry 27 |

## What no amount of research fixes

The gap between you and Don D. is not information. He has sixteen contracts and a one-sentence offer; you have an
artefact nobody on that page can match and no published portfolio item to show it in. Two of those are one afternoon
each, and research is a way of not doing them. The 21-part field guide claim, the empty hours field, the missing video,
and three unpublished portfolio items are all still open, and all of them beat another study.

If you want one thing from me today, ask for the corpus of thirty posts. It is the only item on this list that makes
the proposals better this week rather than next month.
