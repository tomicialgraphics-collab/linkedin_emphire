The fourth one decides the sale, and it has a second half that nobody asks for.

Can you prove a policy was enforced at runtime, and can you prove it to somebody who does not work for
you, a year later, after the person who ran it has left the company?

That isn't a dashboard question. A dashboard is a claim made by the party being audited. What answers it
is a record: what happened, when, who signed for it, and a fingerprint of the evidence, held somewhere the
auditor can check without trusting the system that produced it.

Which is the same test as your first lens. A risk number is also a claim. You taught me this at 1.45 this
morning, so it's your own rule I'm applying to your own post. The 5.56 has a source. The 20 per cent does
not, and the 20 per cent is the one a CFO asks about first.

Show the gap, yes. Naming who can check the arithmetic is the harder half.
