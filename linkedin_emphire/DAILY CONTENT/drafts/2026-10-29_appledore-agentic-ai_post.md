Where the cost of an incident compounds by the minute, what does an AI
system actually have to do?

Appledore Research's new paper, "Agentic AI in Mission-Critical
Operations: Setting a New Standard" (sponsored by Vitria), sets out
five requirements that must all hold at once:

1. Real-time detection and resolution, in seconds to minutes rather
than the hours a cross-team investigation takes
2. Scale across every domain, because a serious incident doesn't stay
in one
3. Correlation across domains, drawing scattered alarms into one
incident
4. Behavioral semantics, knowing whether a dependency is full or
partial and how far impact really spreads
5. Recommendation and validation, checking a fix against the model,
policy and current state before acting

Missing any one of them is how fast automation turns into fast
mistakes. The paper turns each requirement into a threshold you can
test a vendor against, and its last page asks the question I'd want in
any review: can every autonomous action be traced to the relationships
and policies that justified it, in a form that would survive a
technical audit?
