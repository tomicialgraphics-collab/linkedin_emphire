Your test is the right one, and when it fails it usually fails for one specific reason: the trail records what the system did, and the decision rested on what the system was permitted to do.

Those are different rows. An approval, a rule at the version in force, and a refusal are decisions. A successful call is an event. Most trails store events and the file gets called an audit because it is large.

The reconstruction is more useful if you fix the question before you run it. Take the decision, then ask the trail for three things: the rule that authorised it, at the version that was live that day, who had the authority to refuse it, and what was refused in the same window.

The third one is the one that never exists. Refusals are usually not written anywhere, so a decision that was checked and a decision nobody examined leave the same silence, and the trail cannot tell you which you are looking at.

That is the same defect as an empty log read as nothing happened. A clean trail is not evidence of a clean process. It is evidence that the process wrote something down.
