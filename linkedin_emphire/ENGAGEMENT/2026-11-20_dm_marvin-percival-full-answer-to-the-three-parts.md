Full answer, in your order.

Part one. All eight are taken, and two of them changed the record rather than the wording. Keying to the parcel means a deed nobody registered still sits beside the deed that was, and that is the only version of this that survives a forgery. On her number you're simply right. Storing it to say a link was opened defeats the point of not storing it. Link and date, nothing else.

Part two, where I'd add four things and disagree once.

Voice evidence is the strongest idea in the document and the most fragile thing in it, for a reason that isn't in the write-up. A recording is one object on one phone, and if that phone dies, the only account of how the family got the land dies with it. Copying and fingerprinting it fixes that, and it also means we now hold voices and names, which is the heaviest data in the whole product.

Then timing. A recording made three years before a sale and one made after a dispute opens are different evidence, and the record knows the difference because it knows when it was made. So the certificate should say when the telling happened relative to the event, not only when it was uploaded. That costs nothing and it's true.

Spoken consent is right, with one condition. It has to be consent in her words that names what she is allowing: kept, shown to buyers, deleted on request. A tick box under a Yoruba recording is consent to nothing.

Attestation. Four kinds is right, and the load-bearing one is the verifier, which nobody checks. If the person certifying independence is a family friend, every attestation above him is weaker and the page won't say so, so show the verifier's relationship to the parties as well as the witness's. Add one state you'll need within a month of launch: withdrawn. A witness who recants is an event, never an edit, and the certificate carries the withdrawal and its date. A record that can hide a withdrawal is worse than no record.

Coordinates, and this is the disagreement. On a phone, a boundary walk is five to ten metres out, and in a Lagos layout that is the neighbouring plot. So never call coordinates a boundary. Call it a walk of the boundary, on a date, by a named person with a named phone, and store the accuracy the phone reports. That also answers the other problem, which is that whoever holds the phone can walk the wrong line. A walk is an event with an author, not a measurement.

Two smaller ones. If we take anything from the registry, we keep the date we took it, because their record changes and ours won't. And recovery in Stele must not be able to rewrite history. A recovered account can add and never delete, or recovery becomes the way to erase an attestation.

Part three. Your numbers are working numbers, so two questions instead of objections. Who pays at the moment of the sale, since the buyer won't and the agency will, which is what makes the free check work. And should the agency price have a per-deal option, because a small agency doing two or three deals a month won't pay fifteen thousand monthly, and those agencies are most of Lagos.

The order is right, with one thing worth knowing. Build, partners, funding means the signature is on the critical path for iDICE, so the signed surveyor matters more than the working product where the grant is concerned. Pick him early.

The practice build I'll take, with one thing I'd rather say out loud on the call than in a message. Forty-five requirements over four cycles is about eleven a week, so I'd like cycle one named as eleven, and the remaining thirty-four kept as a list we re-order after the first eleven work.

On the name. Ẹ̀rí is a good word and a bad name, because the tone marks are the only thing stopping it meaning the ancestor, and no agency clerk types tone marks. Keep it working, spend nothing more on it, and name the thing when a user has to say it out loud. I can't check the trademark from here, so I won't argue that part either way. On the Igbo friend: I'm Yoruba, and the ask is worth more from you than the guess is from me.

Send the documents and I'll read them before we speak.
