## Reply, paste as is

Sébastien, thank you, and the same to you. I read the site before replying, which is the only fair way to answer a message that quotes my profile.

The line that made me trust the rest of it sits under the hash: that a hash shows an object hasn't changed and doesn't prove authorship, authenticity, truth or certification. Most tools in this space let the reader infer the opposite, and the demo marking authenticity as not observed says the chain has a declared edge rather than a hidden one.

That edge is the whole argument, and it's where I'd push. An evidence file that only its vendor can verify is a report. The hard part isn't producing the record. It's the second reader: someone with no access to your instrument, holding the file, deciding on their own whether it holds. So what do you hand a client whose auditor can't run it? I ask because that fallback is usually where the real design lives.

The failure I meet most often in review work has the same shape, one layer up. Not a wrong result. A true result that nobody outside the team can invoke.

If it's useful I'll send you the case study I finished this week. A client put seven corrections into it, then cleared it with his name on it, which is the closest thing I own to an evidence file that survived a hostile reader.
