Mutual, and unpaid, for this first one.

The reason isn't generosity. If you pay me for it, I'm a vendor reviewing your framework and you'll get a vendor's politeness back. Unpaid and capped means neither of us has a reason to soften the note, and that's the only version worth showing anybody.

Send the contract when it's ready. I'll need access to something running, even a test system, because a test set written against a document says nothing about a deployed one.

## Why not paid, and why say so with the reason

Charging a prospective partner for a proof of concept kills the pairing before it exists, and she has no client to attach it to. Doing it free out of politeness kills the honest note, which is the whole product. Naming the real reason, that payment turns him into a vendor, is the only version of this that survives being asked about later.

## Why the boundary is stated twice

Capped at a day, and the cap holds even if it is going badly. A cap that flexes under pressure is not a cap, and saying so now is cheaper than arguing about it in a week.

## Why the paid condition is named without a number

A fee without a deliverable is a number that gets bargained down. Naming the structure and not the figure keeps the rate intact and puts the price where it belongs, in a written scope with a client attached.

## The access condition

A test set written against a document proves the document. The only claim worth making is about something running, so access is not a courtesy request but the thing the offer depends on.
