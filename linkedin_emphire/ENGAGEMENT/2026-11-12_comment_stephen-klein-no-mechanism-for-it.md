## Comment, paste as is

What I keep circling is why this one lands harder than the rest, and I think it's that he was the one whose claims could be checked. Chips ship or they don't. Revenue gets audited. A roadmap either arrives or it quietly slips. Almost everything else in this industry runs on a story nobody can test until years later, so the person we could check came to stand for the person we could trust.

Those are different variables. Competence tells you someone can build the machine. It has never told anyone what a person does when the machine gives them reach, and no audit answers that question.

That's why a better grownup doesn't solve it. There was never a mechanism for it in the first place.

## The angle I did not take

His post names the Anthropic IPO as the height of the foolishness, and there is a hard, checkable way into that: the same filing has produced headline valuations of roughly 1 trillion, 1.3 trillion and 1.4 trillion, because each count uses a different share base. That is close to a perfect illustration of his own complaint about financial engineering.

It is also the wrong comment to leave here. He wrote about being let down by someone he admired and asked whether any grownups are left. Answering that with a lesson in share counting would be accurate, in lane, and would read as someone who did not listen to the post. The valuation detail stays in this file for a post of his own another day.

## Why this shape

The post is emotional and public, and the register that works under those is a person talking, not a framework. Three short paragraphs, one idea, no agreement phrase, no link, nothing about the industry's finances that he did not raise himself.

The last line answers his closing question without pretending the answer is comfortable, and it does not tell him what to think about the man. It says the thing I actually believe: the industry has no control that converts technical competence into judgement about a person's own exposure, and looking for a better person is not the same as building one.

## If he replies

The conversation worth having is about what such a control would even look like, because the honest answer is that there is no version an organisation can install about a person. That is a real limit, not a design gap, and it is worth saying plainly to someone who just discovered it the hard way.
