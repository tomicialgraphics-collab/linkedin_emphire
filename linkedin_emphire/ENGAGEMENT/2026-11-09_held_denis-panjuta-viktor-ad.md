## Decision: skip the comment

Not because the topic is wrong. Because of three things specific to this post.

It is an advertisement, tagged as a paid partnership, with a credit offer and the link held in the first comment for reach.
Anything written under it is written under the ad, and a substantive comment from a security practitioner reads as an
endorsement of numbers nobody outside that company has audited.

The engagement underneath is a farm. Eleven replies inside ten minutes, nearly all one line and nearly all the same shape:
"Efficiency drives scale", "Automation needs oversight", "Execution matters beyond conversation". A considered paragraph goes
into that pile and comes out sounding like the pile.

And the audience is the wrong one. His readers are people buying a growth system, not people who commission evaluations.

## If he wants the slot used anyway

One line, no endorsement, one question that the vendor cannot answer with a number:

"Approval is the part worth publishing. Of the changes Viktor prepared in those sixty campaigns, what share did the team reject or rewrite, and does that share move when the campaign count doubles? A queue with no rejections is a notification."

That is the disagreement rate again, aimed at the one claim in the post that could be tested. It survives contact with a
vendor comment section because it asks for a figure rather than arguing.

## What this decision is not

It is not a judgement about Viktor as a product, and nothing here says the numbers are false. It says they are unaudited and
that commenting under an ad is how a practitioner accidentally lends a signature.

## Better use of the same effort

The argument belongs in a post of his own, where he controls the frame and the audience is the one that buys assessments.
That post is already queued as the 11 November piece on model judges, and the approval question belongs in its follow up.
