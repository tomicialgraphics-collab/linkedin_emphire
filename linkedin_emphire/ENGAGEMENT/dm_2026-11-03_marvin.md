Read the landing page, then the 4.2 specification and the wire standing table, so this is about the spec rather than the pitch. The version discipline is the part I trust most: accepting 4.0 through 4.2 forever, and never raising a legacy record to 4.3 to judge it, is the right call for evidence that has to outlive its producer.

The part I would push on is completeness. Everything described proves that a record which exists is unchanged since anchoring, and the trust model is honest that truth at origination belongs to the submitter. But what auditors fail companies on is usually the missing record: the side agreement, the unrecorded accrual, the payment that never entered a system. A commitment over what was written says nothing about what wasn't. If a lane anchored an expected count per window, or a per-source sequence, omission would break visibly, the same way tampering does.

Is that in scope, or left to the auditor's other tests? It is the difference between evidence that strengthens a control and evidence that replaces one, and the second is much harder to support.
