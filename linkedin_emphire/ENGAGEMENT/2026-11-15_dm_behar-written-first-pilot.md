# Written reply to Behar on the first pilot, 15 November 2026

What this is: the written continuation he asked for. One use case, one price, one ask. What to do next: the owner
sends it.

## Why this message is longer than the last one

He gave three openings in four lines: no call today, keep going in writing, and agree on one use case plus a
customer conversation. A short friendly reply would waste all three. Writing is also the format where vagueness
is visible, which is why the message commits to a use case, a price and a request.

## DM, paste this

No problem at all. Writing is better for this anyway, because it forces both of us to be concrete.

One use case to start with: an AI agent that takes an action the business cannot undo. A refund above a
threshold, a payout, a change to an account, a message that goes to customers. The failure that costs money is
not a bad answer. It is an action taken with no evidence that the check in front of it either ran or refused.
Today that evidence is usually a dashboard nobody can re-run.

What a customer would need to see before funding a pilot, as I would put it to them: where the action boundary
actually sits in their system, one hostile attempt that the brake refuses, the same refusal re-run byte for byte
so it cannot be dismissed as a demo, a log that survives a restart and a tamper attempt, and then a price and a
date. That last one is what most pilots skip, and it is the only one that tells them whether they would have
bought.

My suggestion on shape: the first one is a paid pilot, two to three weeks, fixed fee of 2,600 dollars for the
first customer. A free proof of concept tells us what they think, not what they will pay, and I need revenue this
quarter rather than a reference. What we build for it should mostly be reusable, so the second customer costs us
half the work of the first.

What I need from you is the part I cannot do: two names from your network, one line each on why they would care.
I will write the customer-facing version of everything above once we know who we are talking to.

Ownership of whatever we reuse is a conversation worth having early, and easier once there is a customer in the
room. The pack stays separate and still lands when the cases are covered.

## Why each part is in there

**The use case is an action, not a model.** Everything that can be sold here depends on an action somebody
regrets. Choosing "agent takes an irreversible action" makes the buyer concrete, puts the price next to a real
loss, and matches what the HFB pack already demonstrates.

**The buyer's list is his own red team discipline pointed at a sale.** Where the boundary sits, one refusal, the
same refusal twice, a log that survives. It doubles as the pilot scope, which is the point: the thing that gets
sold and the thing that gets built are the same list, so a pilot cannot quietly grow.

**The price is named.** 2,600 dollars is the standard security package and two of them is the quarter. Saying it
in the first commercial message also tests whether his contacts are buyers: someone with a real prospect will
engage with a number, and someone with an interesting network will change the subject.

**Ownership is raised, not settled.** A collaboration with a policy researcher can quietly become a co-founder
conversation with an implied split. One clause, no negotiation, and it is easier to finish with a customer in the
room than in a vacuum.

**The ask is two names.** Not five, not "introductions". Two names with one line each is cheap for him, specific
enough to be answered, and impossible to satisfy with a vague yes.

## The risk this message is testing for

If his network is policy and research rather than budget holders, the reply surfaces it in one round instead of in
six weeks. Nothing in the message depends on him having customers, and nothing in it will be wasted if he does
not: the use case and the buyer's list are the sales asset either way.

## Not done here

No equity conversation, no product specification, no deck. The pack and the two technical cases remain a separate
thread on their own schedule.
