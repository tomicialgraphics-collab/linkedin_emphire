## Message, paste as is

Honest answer to your question: internal by default, and mostly it stalls. What I find gets fixed about as often as the team can fix it without asking anyone for authority. The findings that need somebody to decide whether the system may keep running sit there, because the person who could decide is a level above the people who asked me.

Which is your point arriving from the other side. You are right that permission to continue is the real condition, and the reason it is rare is that it belongs to a role, not to a tool.

Now the part where I would rather be straight with you than agreeable. I do not have a bench of clients to send you, so a referral swap would be one-sided today. One engagement is at the merge stage and that is close to the whole of it. What I can offer is the thing you said you are assembling: somebody who tests from outside, on the record.

So here is a version that needs no client at all. Take the narrowest framework you have, the one where permission to continue is already a stop condition rather than a posture. Send me the framework and I will write the tests that would show a deployed system failing to enforce it, run them against whatever you can give me access to, and hand you a short note saying what held and what did not. A day of my time, capped there. If it survives, that pairing is worth showing to a client, and it means something because neither of us certified our own work.

Your side of that offer is the harder one. Pick the framework whose stop condition a stranger could falsify without asking you what it means, and that is the one I would test first.

## Why the refusal comes first

She asked whether clients need outside help and the flattering answer is yes. The true answer is that they mostly do not, and that the ones who do are stuck on authority rather than capability, which is her thesis and makes the refusal more persuasive than a pitch would be.

Saying plainly that there is no client bench costs nothing. She will discover it in a month, and a partner who overstates his pipeline once is a partner whose claims need checking afterwards.

## Why the first project needs no client

Her Drift Triage work is not running live, so a joint delivery is not available and pretending otherwise would put a date on something neither of us controls. A written test set against a framework is available this week, needs one day, and produces the only asset that matters for a second call: evidence that the pairing works on paper before anybody pays for it.

## The closing question

It is the filter. A framework with a sharp stop condition can be tested by a stranger, and one without cannot, which tells me within a paragraph whether her work is falsifiable in the way my reviews have to be. Asking it also hands her the choice of which of her frameworks gets tested first.

## Held back

No fee, no revenue share, no exclusivity. Any of those three before the first artifact exists converts a collaboration into a negotiation about a thing nobody has seen.
