## Comment, paste as is

Thank you, and that's the whole trick really: the review has to still be able to say no.

One addition on the sampling. It should be drawn by somebody who didn't build the agent, or it's a self-review with a calendar attached. Where there's only one person, the next best thing is to write down what you would have rejected and leave the count where it can be seen. A reviewer who hasn't disagreed with the system in a month is the finding, not the reassurance.

The episodes keep landing on things a small team can do on a Monday, which is rarer in this field than it should be.

## Why the sample and not the thanks

She agreed, so agreeing back adds nothing, and her restatement is better than a repeat of the original would be. The one place her sentence can be pushed is the word real, because a review is real when it can produce a no, and the sampling design is where that either holds or quietly stops holding.

Independence is the part owners skip. A sample drawn by the person who wrote the agent is the same failure as a zero reject rate, one step further from being noticed.

## Why the compliment is at the end and specific

The standing note on commercial and creator accounts is that flattery is noise and a specific observation is information. The specific thing is that her guests bring work a small team can start on a Monday, which is a claim about the format rather than about her, and it is said once.

## If she replies

The question worth having is what the sample size should be for internal output, since five a month tells you nothing and fifty is the same work as reading everything. The honest answer is probably that sampling suits briefings and nothing else.
