# Should he be asked for an open price

No, and not in that form.

## Why not

He gave a budget reason, not a price objection. His words were that he has personally funded the development, that the
project runs on independent researchers and contributors, and that he is no longer in a position to finance external
audits. That is a statement about an empty account, and asking him to name a number turns a clean no into a negotiation
with somebody who has just told you there is nothing in it.

There are only two ways it ends. He names something near zero, and either the week gets done at that number, which prices
a week of security work at a fraction of a day rate and teaches the next client to haggle, or the offer is refused, which
means the exchange happened for nothing. Or he goes quiet, which is what usually follows being asked twice.

Open pricing works where the client has money and disagrees with the number. It is the wrong instrument where the client
has told you there is no money.

There is a second reason specific to this project. NeoMundi pays external people in attribution, and he has already offered
that in writing. Asking for a number in that setting invites exposure to arrive as the counter-offer, and it puts the
reviewer in the position of negotiating against his own independence.

## What to do instead

Leave the $1,500 where it is, take the free write-up and the reviewer listing, and stop asking. The piece is the asset: it
is the second case study, it names two defects in somebody else's verifier, and it will be there in December when the
question is what has been done rather than what has been quoted.

If a smaller paid unit is wanted, offer one, with a floor, once:

"If a smaller piece is payable, the two findings written up as a review memo is $450 and it takes two days. If that isn't
payable either, say so and the free write-up stands. I won't ask a third time."

That has a price in it, so it is not a request to be given one. It also ends the subject either way.

## What not to do

No "what would you consider fair", no "whatever fits your budget", no payment plan, no revenue share, no equity. Each of
these converts a relationship worth keeping into a discount negotiation, and none of them produces money this month.
