The AI read plain and drew a plane.

Four Just Eat menu items, four regional words. This failure ships first wherever the menu is not in the model's training data.

Just Eat spent the week apologising, and the apology is more interesting than the error. It had been trialling AI images for dishes with no photo on the menu. What came back: a chip muffin as muffins with potato chips baked in. A cheese roll as cheese rolled into sushi. A bacon and egg soda bread as a glass of cola with an egg and bacon floating in it. Garlic bread shaped like a plane, where the ticket said plain.

Here's what most teams get wrong about this:

They file it as a model quality problem and wait for the next model.

The reality is different.

Every one of those came from a correct reading of a word that only makes sense locally. It wasn't confused. What it produced was accurate about the wrong sense of the word, which is the failure mode that never announces itself.

Here's what actually works:

→ Keep a glossary of the local terms in your catalogue, with the dish each one means. Cheapest control in the class, and almost nobody has one.

→ Check the output against the noun in the item name, not against whether it looks plausible. A competent image of cola with bacon in it passes plausible.

→ Route low-confidence readings to a person who can see the description and has standing to reject it.

The part nobody tells you:

This arrives first where the vocabulary was never in the training data. Manchester calls it a chip muffin. Lagos has a longer list, and it is not in the corpus. Teams hit first are the ones furthest from where the model was built, and they get hit while the tool is still called a pilot.

A better model narrows this. It doesn't close it, because the next menu has its own words.
