## Why it reads this way

**Two days, one window, no menu.** A person asking for a call is deciding whether you are easy to schedule. Giving a range and asking him to name the hour is easier to answer than four options, and it puts the timezone conversion on him, where it belongs, because he knows his own clock.

**The 20 minutes.** It caps the call before it is booked. An unbounded call with a stranger in a different timezone is the one that gets rescheduled.

**The last line is the useful one.** He answered a question about where evidence breaks with a request for a call, which is a fair move and tells you nothing. Asking whether the call is about his client or his own tooling separates a referral conversation from a sales conversation, and it lets him answer in six words without committing to either.

## If he answers

**A client.** That is the referral path, and the only thing to establish is who decides and what they would pay to have the claim tested. Do not quote a fee on the first call.

**His own measurement.** He is building ROI claims at IBM. The offer there is the audit, not the engagement: a fixed-price read of one number and the evidence under it, delivered as a page he can forward inside the account.

**Neither, and he wants to sell to you.** Possible, given the GTM title. Twenty minutes is enough to find out, and the answer is the same one sent to every inbound pitch this month: no spend before the first invoice.
