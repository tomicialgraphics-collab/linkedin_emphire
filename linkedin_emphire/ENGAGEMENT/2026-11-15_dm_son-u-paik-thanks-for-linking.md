## Why the reply gives before it asks

"Thanks for linking" is a door being held open, not a conversation. Answering it with a pleasantry closes the door politely and nothing happens, which is the fate of most accepted connections.

So the first line says what you do in the terms that make him place himself: red team, tests published, agent as the object. The second half of that sentence offers him a category that is not competitive, because an auditor who writes conformity documents and an engineer who breaks agents want the same client for different reasons.

Then one question, narrow enough to answer in six words. He does not have to explain his business or justify his existence, which is what makes it get answered.

## The two shapes this relationship can take

**If he audits and needs the testing underneath.** That is the best outcome on this thread. AI audits increasingly require evidence, and evidence is the part most auditors cannot produce themselves. He becomes a referral source with a repeat need, and the sales conversation never starts cold.

**If he writes the conformity and governance documents.** Then the joint offer writes itself for the EU-facing tier already in the ICP: he supplies the Article 50 transparency and governance paper, the red-team report supplies the technical evidence under it. Neither of you can sell the whole thing alone, and the buyer wants one document.

Either way this is the gap in the current services page. The audit sells technical evidence and stops at the edge of legal and regulatory language, which is exactly where a regulated buyer's procurement starts. A partner who covers that edge makes the offer complete without either of you learning the other's trade.

## What not to do on the next message

Do not send the services page. Do not price anything. Do not offer a call in the second message either: he has not yet said which half of the headline he occupies, and a calendar link before that is a pitch wearing a friendly hat.
