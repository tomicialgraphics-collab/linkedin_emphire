## Decision

Do not comment here. Hold the line for Anthropic's own post if it appears, or for a practitioner who describes actually running one of these.

Three reasons, in order of weight:

1. **The comment would land on a reseller, not on the company making the claim.** Anthropic's guide is the thing being discussed, and the audience on a one-minute repost by a small account is close to nobody.
2. **The room is the wrong room.** Sales operations and GTM founders are not the people who buy evaluation and red team work. Being right in front of them costs attention and returns none.
3. **There is nothing to answer.** A repost has no author argument in it. A comment under one is a broadcast, and broadcasts from people who usually add something read as noise.

## The line worth holding

If Anthropic's own post appears, or if someone posts about running one of these tools in anger, this is the comment. It echoes their campaign line without arguing with the product, and it is the question a finance reader will wish somebody had asked.

```
Formula-ready is the part that worries me. A spreadsheet is the one artefact in a sales org where a wrong assumption does not throw an error. It flows through every downstream cell and shows up in a board number eight weeks later.

If Claude writes the model, the useful ask is not the formula. It is the assumption list: which cells are inputs, which are judgements, and which one it chose on your behalf.

For every sales task there is Claude. For every generated model there should be a page that says what it assumed.
```

## Why the last line works

It borrows their own construction and turns it into a condition rather than a complaint, so it reads as an addition to the campaign instead of an objection to it. That is also why it should not be spent on a repost: it is the best sentence available on this topic this week, and it deserves the vendor's own comment thread.

## Not to be reused

The containment-error framing from the Walisko comment. Same class of mistake, already used once, and repeating a frame teaches the audience that there is only one.
