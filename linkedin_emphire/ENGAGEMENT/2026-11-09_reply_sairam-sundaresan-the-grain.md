## The angle

Move from whether to report by segment, which is now agreed, to at what grain, which is where the advice runs into arithmetic. A
series with thirty observations cannot support a coverage estimate, so a per SKU table over forty thousand items is noise wearing
the clothes of diligence. Pooling into volume bands, or a declared top list, is what gives each cell enough points to mean
anything.

Then the sequencing rule, which is the part that protects the table from the people who commission it: fix the cuts before the
results arrive.

## Reply, paste as is

The grain is where this becomes practical. Coverage per series is uninformative on a series with thirty observations, so a table across forty thousand SKUs is noise dressed as diligence. Pool into volume bands, or a fixed top list, where each cell holds enough points to mean something.

Then fix the cuts before the results arrive. A segment table written after the fact tends to contain the segments that looked acceptable, and nobody does that deliberately.
