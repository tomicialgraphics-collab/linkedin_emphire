## Why it says so little

The email asked for one thing, in one sentence, and gave a same-day deadline that has now passed. A short reply that names the address, confirms the spam check and says the rest is in hand is the whole of what a programme team needs to resend an invitation. Anything longer invites a question about why it was not reported on the day.

**"I am writing from that address"** does the work that matters. Their process says to reply from the application address so it can be matched to the file, and it also proves the mailbox is live.

## Timing

Send it today if the invitation is missing. They aim to answer within three business days, and the deadline is 13 days out, so a same-week fix is comfortably inside the window. Discovering this on 26 November would not be.

## The order of work, which the email order hides

The three tasks look equal because they share a deadline, and they are not equal.

1. **References.** Binary. All three must be in or the application does not advance, and two are still with other people who have had the form for a day. This is the only item that cannot be fixed by working harder.
2. **The takehome.** This is where research taste is judged, it is the item with five hours attached, and the two papers they named are the only preparation that cannot be done inside the clock.
3. **CodeSignal.** Two hours, no minimum score, and the email says outright that applicants with poor scores have been hired when they were strong elsewhere. It gets one clear sitting and a compatibility check, not a weekend of drills.
