# Reply to Paul Sheals, 8 November 2026

## What he did

He took the question seriously and answered it with a split rather than a percentage: brands recognised at turn one, lost at a
logged turn, with the counterfactual experiment on top. That is more than a marketing series usually carries, and the reply
should concede it cleanly instead of hunting for a new objection.

There is a real second observation available, though, and it is better than the first one I made. His own linkage work says
most of what a model knows about a brand never gets deployed. If that is true, then using an injected fact ten times out of
ten does not show the fact was missing. It shows that delivery at the decision turn is the variable, which moves most of the
remediation from copy to plumbing.

## Reply

That is a better answer than the question asked for, and the turn-level log settles it.

The part that stands out is how it sits next to the linkage finding. If most of what a model knows about a brand never gets
deployed, then using the fact ten times out of ten is not evidence the fact was missing. It is evidence that delivery at the
decision turn is the variable. Which makes most of that remediation bucket an API problem rather than a copy problem: price
and fit have to be reachable and parseable at the moment the buyer names their situation, not described more persuasively
on a page.

The two stated-fit cases are the honest part of the result.

## What not to say

No doubt about the sample size of ten, since he has already flagged the two that failed and the two that failed make the
result credible rather than weaker. No restating his numbers back at him as agreement, which adds nothing in a thread where
he wrote them. No mention of my own work.

## Held for later

The question worth a proper exchange, not a comment: whether an agent that cannot verify a price is cutting the brand or
cutting the fact, and whether publishing a machine-readable price changes the cut rate at all. That is a measurable
experiment and it is the one his Part 4 needs.
